# Corebridge X — Product Brief

Locked decisions, kept here so any fresh session has context without re-deriving it from chat history. If something here turns out to be wrong, update this file in the same change that changes the decision.

## What this is

An internal PSA (project + team-ops) tool for **Corebridge X**, a B2B outsourcing hub serving several partner brands: **Sparing Consulting, EdgeNovelty, Bill Optimum, VeroTax Advisory, Croki Digital**. It is **client-company-centered** — companies/clients are the hub every other entity (contacts, workstreams, tasks, notes, documents) hangs off of. As of Phase 2.0, work is structured as **Company → Workstream → Task**: a workstream is one service delivered to one client (e.g. "Payroll 2026"), and every task belongs to exactly one workstream.

It is **fully internal**:
- Only Corebridge X staff ever log in. Accounts are **invite-only** — there is no self-signup.
- **Client Contacts are reference-only.** They are data *about* a client's people (name, title, email, phone), never accounts. They never log in and there is no client portal.

## Roles (RBAC)

Three roles, permission rules defined once in `src/lib/data/permissions.ts` and reused everywhere (screens, providers, and — later — Supabase RLS as a defense-in-depth backstop, not the source of truth):

- **Employee** — sees only their own assigned tasks and their assigned companies; ticks tasks done; writes notes; tracks time; can self-add a task that goes live immediately (no approval) and notifies their supervisor + superadmin; generates their own reports.
- **Supervisor** — everything an employee can do, plus creates/assigns tasks to their team, sees their team's tasks/companies/progress/profiles, and views/generates their team's reports. "Their team" = their direct reports (single supervisor per employee, via `supervisorId`).
- **Superadmin** — everything above across the whole org, plus org-wide counts (clients/staff/tasks), manages/invites accounts, and views every role's reports.

**Company visibility gate** (locked in Phase 1a): a user's `assignedCompanyIds` is the primary scope.
- Employee sees companies in their own `assignedCompanyIds`.
- Supervisor sees the union of `assignedCompanyIds` across themselves and their direct reports.
- Superadmin sees all companies.

Company **create/edit** is restricted to supervisor + superadmin — employees have read-only access to their assigned companies.

**Task visibility gate** (locked in Phase 1b): employee sees tasks where they're an assignee (and the task's company is one they can access); supervisor sees tasks where *any* assignee is on their team; superadmin sees all. Only supervisor/superadmin can assign a task to someone else — employees can only self-assign. A special **Internal / Non-billable** pseudo-company exists for non-client work and is always selectable by every active staff member, without being added to anyone's `assignedCompanyIds`.

Time tracking and note-writing follow the *same* task/company visibility gate — there is no separate permission axis for them (see below).

## Workstreams (locked in Phase 2.0; corrected to a lightweight service container in Phase 3.34)

*Terminology note: this layer was originally called "Engagement" when built in Phase 2.0. It was renamed to "Workstream" after Phase 2.1/1.5c shipped, once the term tested poorly with stakeholders — the concept, data model, relationships, and permissions are unchanged; only the name is different. If you're reading older design notes or commit history that says "Engagement," it means this.*

Inserted a layer between Company and Task: **Company → Workstream → Task**. A workstream represents one service delivered to one client (e.g. "Payroll 2026," "Tax Advisory Workstream Proposal"), and groups a client's work by the service it belongs to instead of one flat pile of tasks per company. **A client has always been able to have multiple workstreams** (Cinderpoint Logistics, seeded with both a Payroll and a Bookkeeping workstream, has demonstrated this since Phase 2.0) — investigated in Phase 3.33 after a report that "Add Workstream" seemed broken, which traced not to a data/listing bug but to `WorkstreamFormDialog` redirecting straight to the newly-created workstream's own detail page on create, so the company page's own updated list was never actually seen. Fixed there — see "Time UI Clarity + Workstream/Activity Hierarchy" below.

A Workstream has: name, an optional freeform description (since Phase 3.21, same treatment as `Task.description`), company, service line (nullable — see below), partner brand, a lead + team, status (`active` / `on-hold` / `completed` / `cancelled`), start date and a **renewal date** (both optional — an ongoing service has no single "due date"; see below), a real recurrence cadence since Phase 3.19 (see "Recurring Work" below — `Task.recurrenceRule` remains the untouched opaque placeholder), and a derived overall progress (percentage of its tasks with status `done`). **It deliberately carries no time/effort field of its own** — see "Workstream as Service Container" (Phase 3.34) below for why, and where that comparison actually lives now.

**Data-model decision**: `Task.companyId` still exists and is still what every existing screen/provider reads — but it's now a *denormalized* value, synced from `workstream.companyId` by the provider whenever a task is created or edited, never independently editable in the UI. This kept `canAccessTask`, the time-entries provider, and every company-scoped task query working unchanged; only task creation/editing changed (pick a workstream, not a company — company is derived).

**Workstream visibility gate** (mirrors the task/company gates): employee sees workstreams where they're the lead or on the team; supervisor sees workstreams where their lead or any team member is on their own team (via `managesUser`); superadmin sees all. The **Internal Operations** workstream (scoped to the Internal/Non-billable pseudo-company) is always visible to everyone, mirroring how the Internal company itself is always accessible.

Workstream **create/edit is supervisor + superadmin only** — no employee self-service, unlike self-added tasks.

**Real behavior change from Phase 1.5**: because task creation now requires picking a workstream, self-added tasks are scoped to workstreams the employee is staffed on (plus the always-available Internal workstream) — previously any of an employee's `assignedCompanyIds` was fair game. Existing workstreams were backfilled with team membership derived from each company's pre-Phase-2.0 `assignedCompanyIds`, so no one lost access to work they could already see; going forward, supervisors need to staff new hires onto the right workstream(s).

## Templates (locked in Phase 2.1)

A **Template** captures one workstream's worth of repeatable work — name, service line, a recurrence cadence (real and structured since Phase 3.19 — see "Recurring Work" below; previously an opaque "monthly"/"quarterly" label), and an ordered set of **TemplateTasks**, each with a title, default checklist items, a **default owner role** (a hint like "employee" or "supervisor," shown when applying — not auto-assigned), and a **relative due-date offset** (days after the workstream's start date).

Seed-only for MVP — there is no in-app template create/edit UI yet; that's a later phase. Three templates ship: **Payroll Client Onboarding** (one-time), **Quarterly Tax Filing** (recurring label "quarterly"), **Monthly Bookkeeping** (recurring label "monthly").

**Applying a template** (supervisor/superadmin only, from the company detail page): pick a template, name the workstream (auto-suggested as "Template name — Company (period)," editable), set a start date, pick a lead + team — then a **preview step shows exactly what will be created** (the workstream summary, and every task with its calculated due date, suggested-owner hint, and checklist count) before anything commits. Confirming calls `workstreamsProvider.createWorkstream` once, then `tasksProvider.createTask` once per template task, in order — reusing both providers' existing create paths and access checks rather than a separate write path.

**Tasks are created unassigned**, not auto-assigned by the suggested role — the supervisor distributes them to the real team from the workstream page afterward. This required two small, additive extensions:
- `TaskInput` gained an opt-in `allowUnassigned` flag (only set by the apply-template flow) so a supervisor/superadmin creating a task with zero assignees gets a genuinely unassigned task instead of the normal fallback-to-creator behavior every other task-creation path still uses.
- `canAccessTask`'s supervisor branch was extended so a **fully unassigned task is visible to any supervisor who can access its company** (previously a supervisor only saw tasks with at least one teammate assigned) — otherwise newly-applied-template tasks would be invisible to the very person who needs to triage and assign them.

`Task.templateId` (previously an always-null stub) now records which `TemplateTask` a task was instantiated from, for future traceability/reporting.

**Expected hours (locked in Phase 3.3, storage superseded in Phase 3.31, workstream-level field removed in Phase 3.34 — see below)**: each `TemplateTask` carries an optional expected-time estimate (all three seed templates have realistic per-task values — e.g. Quarterly Tax Filing is 4h/6h/3h across its three tasks). Applying a template copies each task's estimate onto the created `Task`; the apply-template preview still shows the running total next to the task count (a plain sum of what's about to be created), but **as of Phase 3.34 that total is informational only** — it no longer gets written onto the workstream itself (that field doesn't exist anymore), since the workstream's own time-vs-budget is a roll-up of its tasks' estimates, and the real total appears there automatically the moment the tasks exist. Each task's own estimate is directly editable outside of templates too — non-template work can carry a time budget the same way. **As of Phase 3.31, this field is named `expectedMinutes` and stores minutes, not hours** — a plain number field became a value+unit control (minutes/hours/days) at the point of entry; see "Task Quick Refinements" below for the full change.

## Data layer

All data access goes through a swappable provider layer under `src/lib/data/providers/` — screens never call a backend SDK directly.

- `mock/` — in-memory seed data, used today. Filters results using the same `permissions.ts` rules a real backend would enforce, so screens behave identically once swapped.
- `supabase/` — real backend, implemented by a separate team. Stubbed today; swapping is an env var (`NEXT_PUBLIC_DATA_PROVIDER=supabase`) plus filling in the provider methods — no screen rewrites.
- Shared TypeScript types in `src/lib/data/types/` mirror the agreed database schema and are the contract both providers implement against.

## Time tracking (locked in Phase 1c; Pause/Resume added in Phase 3.32)

- **Only one running timer per user at a time.** Starting a new timer auto-pauses (Phase 3.32; previously auto-stopped) whatever timer that user currently has running — on any task, not just the one they're viewing. A time entry counts as "running" whenever `durationMinutes` is `null`; no separate "is running" flag was needed.
- Manual time entries support either a time range (start/end) or a bare duration, plus an optional note.
- **Every time entry is billable by default; entries logged against the Internal/Non-billable company default to non-billable.** The flag is a plain checkbox on the entry, so it can always be overridden either direction (e.g. comping otherwise-billable work).
- Time entries always record who logged them — this attribution is what the later superadmin "who did what" report will read from directly, no extra bookkeeping needed.

## Notes (locked in Phase 1c)

Notes are always internal-only and come in two independent kinds, never mixed in the same list:
- **Task notes** — comments on a task's detail page.
- **Company notes** — commentary on a company's detail page.

Each note has an author, a timestamp, and a type (`call` / `meeting` / `internal` / `decision`). Writing a note requires only the same view access as the task/company itself — it is **not** manager-gated, matching the base employee capability "writes notes on tasks" from the original RBAC brief.

## Task Handoffs (locked in Phase 1.5c)

A structured (not free-text) record of "where I left off" passed between people on a task: handed-by (always the acting user), handed-to, work done so far, work remaining, optional blockers, and a timestamp. The recipient **acknowledges** it (recording who and when); a task's full handoff history is shown on its detail page, newest first — same list-building convention as notes.

**Who can hand off, and to whom**: creating a handoff requires only the same view access as the task itself (`canCreateHandoff` — literally an alias of `canAccessTask`, not manager-gated, matching the Notes philosophy above). The recipient picker is restricted server-side to people who can **already** access the task — handing off doesn't grant new access; reassigning who's actually on a task is still done through the existing task-edit flow. Only the recipient can acknowledge their own handoff, and only once.

Creating a handoff pushes a notification to the recipient through the existing notification feed (`type: "task-handoff"`), the same "push straight onto `db.notifications`" pattern every other notification-generating action already uses — no separate handoff-notification API.

**Two small extensions this required**, both additive and scoped only to this feature:
- `NotificationType` gained a `"task-handoff"` member (no code elsewhere switches exhaustively on this type, so this was a safe, non-breaking addition).
- No changes to `canAccessTask`/task assignment were needed — handoffs are purely an additional record on top of the existing task, not a reassignment mechanism.

## Activity Catalog (locked in Phase 2.2; scoped to a workstream's own service in Phase 3.33)

Foundation for the reporting feature that comes next — not a reporting feature itself.

Each partner brand owns its own catalog **independently**: **Brand → Department → Activity**, both `Department` and `Activity` ordered entities (`position`, same convention as `ChecklistItem`/`TemplateTask`). A department belongs to exactly one brand; nothing is shared or inherited across brands. **Only Sparing Consulting's catalog is populated** — 5 departments (Human Resources, Payroll, Accounting, Compliance, File Management) and 78 activities, seeded word-for-word from the source list. EdgeNovelty, Bill Optimum, VeroTax Advisory, and Croki Digital all start with **zero departments** — the schema already supports them, they're just empty until populated. The Internal/Non-billable pseudo-brand gets no catalog at all (it isn't client-service work). **As of Phase 3.33**, each of Sparing's 5 departments also carries its own `serviceLineId` (Human Resources→Human Resources, Payroll→Payroll, Accounting→Accounting, Compliance→Compliance, File Management→File Management — three new service lines were added specifically to complete this 1:1 mapping) — see "Time UI Clarity + Workstream/Activity Hierarchy" below for what that unlocks.

**Task tagging**: `Task.activityId` is a single optional nullable FK — never required, matching "untagged work is fine, it'll fall under 'Other' in reports later." `TaskWithRelations.activity` is a light reference `{id, name, departmentName}` — it needs `departmentName` (unlike the bare `{id, name}` used for `workstream`) because **every department ends in its own "Other"**, so the activity name alone isn't legible without its parent department.

**Brand-scoped resolution, narrowed to the workstream's own service since Phase 3.33**: a task's activity picker resolves the brand via **task → workstream → company → brand** (the workstream's `brand` is already available from `useWorkstreams()`, no extra fetch) exactly as before — but now also passes the workstream's own `serviceLineId` to `listDepartments`, which narrows the result to just the one department mapped to that service line (falling back to the full brand catalog when the workstream has no service line set, since there's nothing narrower to scope to). A brand (or service) with an empty catalog simply shows a "no activities set up for this brand/service yet" disabled state — work is never blocked. The "+ Add Service"/"+ Add section" report dialogs (Phase 2.9/3.25) are unaffected — they still browse a whole brand's catalog, calling `listDepartments` without a service line, exactly as before.

**Simple keyword suggestion**: when a title is entered, a dead-simple token-overlap match (lowercase, strip punctuation, drop stopwords/short words, count overlapping words against each brand-scoped activity's name, take the highest-scoring match) surfaces as a dismissible "Suggested: {Department}: {Activity} — Apply / dismiss" banner next to the picker. Never auto-applied, never shown once an activity is already picked, and dismissing one suggestion doesn't suppress a *different* one that emerges from further title edits. No fuzzy matching, no ranking model — this is intentionally the simplest thing that could plausibly help.

**Permissions**: `listDepartments` is ungated reference data, same treatment as `listBrands()`/`listServiceLines()` — needed so an employee tagging their own self-added task can populate the picker. Setting/clearing a task's tag reuses `canEditTask` as-is (just another editable field, no new permission function). The "supervisor/superadmin view" instruction from planning applies to a future dedicated catalog-browsing/management screen, which isn't built yet — that's where a real view/edit permission gets added, next to the screen that needs it, matching how in-app template editing was deferred the same way in Phase 2.1.

**Not built this phase** (deliberately deferred, not scope-cut): no Activity column/filter on the tasks list, Kanban board, or My Day; no tagging on Templates/TemplateTasks; no dedicated catalog-browsing page; no report logic. All of that is either reporting-adjacent (the next phase this unblocks) or outside "catalog + optional tag."

## Reuse Past Work (locked in Phase 2.7)

Task-level, on-demand automation: when creating or editing a task tagged to an Activity, an optional **"Reuse from past"** button appears next to the Activity field. Clicking it opens a small picker listing the up-to-5 most recently completed tasks tagged to that *same activity* — title, client company, completion date, checklist item count — sourced via a new `TasksProvider.listPastTasksForActivity(viewer, activityId, excludeTaskId?)` method that filters through the exact same `canAccessTask` gate as `listTasks`/`getTask`, so a viewer only ever sees candidates they could already access. Because an Activity is itself brand-scoped (it belongs to a department, which belongs to exactly one brand — see "Activity Catalog" above), filtering candidates by `activityId` alone already keeps results within the same brand; no separate brand filter was needed.

Selecting a candidate **replaces** the current checklist draft with fresh, unchecked copies of that task's checklist item descriptions (new IDs — they become genuinely new items on save, not links back to the old task), plus an "Also copy description" checkbox (unchecked by default) for optionally overwriting the description too. This is a deliberate replace, not a merge — the button is opt-in and explicit ("start from the past version instead of scratch"), so silently merging with whatever the user had already typed would be more surprising than simply replacing. Nothing is auto-applied: the picker only opens on click, and copying only happens when the user picks a specific candidate — the title is never touched, and the checklist/description remain fully editable afterward before saving.

If an activity has no past completed examples, the picker simply shows "No past examples yet for this activity" — the button itself is never hidden or disabled once an activity is tagged (checking in advance whether any exist would mean an extra fetch on every activity change just to decide button visibility), and task creation is never blocked either way.

**Not built this phase**: whole-workstream reuse (copying an entire past workstream's task set at once) — explicitly deferred to a possible later phase, this is task-level only.

## Reports (two views)

1. **Internal reports** — full attribution (staff names, timestamps), flows user → supervisor → superadmin. Built in Phase 2.3 as the **Accomplishments Report** — see below. Unlike the original placeholder framing, this is a **stored snapshot once finalized**, not purely computed on every view.
2. **Client-facing reports** — aggregate only. Must never join through to Internal Users or name any employee. Filtered by company + date range, exportable to PDF/CSV. Built in Phase 3.25 as **Client Reports** — see below. A **separate type/provider**, deliberately not blended with the Accomplishments Report above, which stays fully attributed.

## Accomplishments Report (locked in Phase 2.3)

Replaces a manual paper form ("Account Manager Weekly Accomplishments") with a department → activity checklist, auto-drafted from work already tracked in the app, then freely editable before finalizing. Internal-only, fully attributed — the client-facing Client Reports below is a distinct type/provider, not a variant of this one.

**Data model**: `AccomplishmentsReport` is a **stored snapshot document** (`src/lib/data/types/accomplishments-report.ts`), not a live query. `kind: "person" | "client"` with `subjectId` being a userId or companyId respectively; `rangeStart`/`rangeEnd` (inclusive ISO dates) cover both weekly and daily via the same engine, no special-casing per grain. The editable content is `brandSections: AccomplishmentsReportBrandSection[]` — one section per brand touched, each with `departments` (as of Phase 3.29, only departments with ≥1 auto-detected activity, plus any added by hand via "+ Add service" — a shown department still renders every one of its own catalog activities as a row, ticked or not) and a trailing `other` catch-all line for untagged work under that brand, gated by a sibling `otherIncluded` boolean. Each line also carries a `companyLabel` (distinct client name(s) behind its matched work, briefly listed if several) — only rendered on person reports, since a client report is already scoped to one client. `subjectLabel`/`generatedByName` are snapshotted display names — a later rename never rewrites a past report's header. `status: "draft" | "finalized"`; finalizing sets `finalizedAt` and freezes the report's entries forever (`updateDraft`/`finalizeReport` are rejected once finalized). **Generating never mutates an existing report** — every "Generate" click creates a brand-new row, so there's no merge-into-existing-draft logic and no risk of a regenerate silently overwriting manual edits.

**Ownership and Trash (locked in Phase 2.4, tightened in Phase 2.5)**: a report's "owner" is the one person it's about — the subject themself for a person report, or the generator for a client report (which has no single subject-person). Only the owner can edit entries (tick/untick, rewrite detail) or finalize, and only while still a draft (`canEditAccomplishmentsReportEntries`). Deleting moves a report to a soft-delete state (`deletedAt` timestamp) instead of hard-removing it — works on draft or finalized, gated by the same view-access permission as generate/view (not owner-only, so a supervisor/superadmin can trash a report they can see). `/dashboard/reports/trash` lists trashed reports with Restore and a confirmed "Delete forever" (true hard delete, rejected unless already trashed). **The 30-day auto-purge is only a display computation today** (`deletedAt + 30 days`, shown as "Auto-deletes on…") — there's no scheduled job in the mock backend; wiring an actual purge cron is deferred to the real (Supabase) backend.

**Role-scoped view structure and reviewer comments (locked in Phase 2.5)** — this replaced an earlier, looser design that had a real bug: an employee assigned to a client company could open *any* accomplishments report for that company (including ones they didn't generate) via `canAccessCompany`, and would see the reviewer-comment box on it. The fix is structural, not a screen-level patch:

- `canViewAccomplishmentsReport(viewer, report, allUsers)` is now the **single view gate**, used by `listReports`/`listTrashedReports`/`getReport`/trash/restore/permanent-delete. Owners always see their own. **Employees see only their own, full stop** — the function returns `false` for anything else, so an employee can never open another person's report through any route, including typing the URL directly. Supervisors additionally see reports owned by their direct reports; superadmins see every report.
- A **person report is now always about yourself** — there is no "generate a person report for someone else" anymore (removed the subject picker entirely; `generateReport` ignores/overrides any client-supplied `subjectId` for `kind: "person"` and forces it to the viewer). This closes the confusing case from Phase 2.4 where a report's owner and its generator could differ for person-kind reports. A client report still has no "self," so generating one still reuses `canAccessCompany` — its owner is whoever generated it.
- `/dashboard/reports` is now **two sections, not one flat list**: employees see just their own reports (no tab, since there's nothing else to show). Supervisors get "My Reports" / "Team Reports" (reports owned by their direct reports). Superadmins get "My Reports" / "All Reports" (everyone else's). The split is a client-side partition of an already-correctly-scoped list (`isAccomplishmentsReportOwner`, which needs no `allUsers`) — the permission layer, not the screen, is what actually prevents an employee from ever fetching anyone else's report in the first place.
- `canCommentOnAccomplishmentsReport` is restricted to **supervisors (their team's reports) and superadmins (any report)** — never the owner commenting on their own work. Posting a comment pushes a `"report-comment"` notification (`AppNotification.relatedReportId`) to the report's owner through the existing notification feed — e.g. "Priya Nair commented on your weekly accomplishments report" — and clicking it lands the owner on the report with the comment visible.

**Correction (Phase 2.6)**: an earlier version of this hid the entire comment thread from employees, even on their own report — so the owner got notified a comment existed but could never actually read it. That was wrong: the `ReportComments` component always renders when there are comments to show *or* the viewer can write one (`canComment`); only the **compose control** is gated to non-owner supervisors/superadmins. The owner — any role — always reads their own report's comments read-only; they just can never write one (there's no one for them to review).

**Reopen and integrity history (Phase 2.6)**: a finalized report is locked, but its owner can **Reopen** it back to `status: "draft"` to correct it and re-finalize — always the *same* report, never a duplicate for the same subject/period. `canReopenAccomplishmentsReport(viewer, report)` requires `status === "finalized"` **and** `isAccomplishmentsReportOwner` — deliberately *not* `canEditAccomplishmentsReportEntries` (that function requires `status === "draft"`, the opposite state). This is owner-only regardless of role: a supervisor/superadmin viewing someone else's finalized report has no Reopen button at all, even though they can view and comment — their only lever on someone else's report is a comment, and the owner reopens and fixes it themselves. Every finalize and reopen appends to `history: AccomplishmentsReportHistoryEvent[]` (`{ type: "finalized" | "reopened" | "re-finalized", actorId, actorName, createdAt }`) — the event type is decided at write time (`finalizeReport` checks whether an earlier `"finalized"`/`"re-finalized"` event already exists and picks the label accordingly), not inferred at render time. `ReportHistory` renders this as a plain, read-only list — "Finalized by Alicia Chen · Jul 27, 3:00 PM", "Reopened by Alicia Chen · Jul 28, 9:00 AM", "Re-finalized by Alicia Chen · Jul 28, 10:00 AM" — visible to anyone who can view the report. **Event log only, no version-diffing or approval workflow.**

**Save feedback and unsaved-changes guard (Phase 2.4)**: saving shows a brief "Changes saved" toast (`src/components/ui/toast.tsx`, a thin Tailwind wrapper around Base UI's `Toast` primitive — already a project dependency, so no new package). `useUnsavedChangesGuard` (`src/lib/data/hooks/`) tracks a local `isDirty` flag on the report detail page: a native `beforeunload` prompt covers tab close/refresh, and a capture-phase click listener intercepts in-app link navigation (sidebar, back link) to offer Save-and-leave / Discard / Cancel instead of silently losing edits. Scoped to this one page, not a global router guard.

**Resolving "untagged work" and "no department" from the spec** into one 3-tier fallback: (1) tagged to a real activity → that activity's row; (2) untagged, under a company with a brand → that brand section's own `other` row (sibling to its departments, not nested in one) — this also covers "brands with empty catalogs show only Other," since a brand section with zero departments renders as just `other`; (3) there's no case (3) in practice — every company (including Internal, brand `brand-internal`) has a brandId, so Internal work is simply its own brand section showing only `other`, not a separate global bucket.

**Auto-draft / "work done" criteria**: broader than "task completed" — a task counts as touched in range if it has **any of**: a checklist item completed in range, time logged in range, or a status change in range (`Task.statusChangedAt`). For a person report, each of those is attributed to that specific person (`completedById`/time entry `userId`/`statusChangedById`); for a client report, attribution doesn't matter — any actor counts, scoped to that company's tasks. The detail text is a deterministic concatenation (task title + total hours logged in range + time-entry notes + task notes in range) — plain string-building, not a summarization model, matching the Activity Catalog's "dead simple" precedent.

**Brand-section inclusion**: a client report always shows exactly one section (the company's own brand), even with zero matching work that period — mirrors the paper form always showing the full checklist. A person report only includes a brand section where they have ≥1 touched task — avoids dumping an irrelevant brand's full catalog in front of someone who never touched it. **Superseded at the department level by Phase 3.29**: a *department* within an included brand section is no longer always the brand's full catalog — only touched departments auto-include, plus any added by hand via "+ Add service." See "'+ Add Service' — Manual Sections on the Internal Report" below. (The brand-section-level rule described here — one section per touched/force-included brand — is unchanged.)

**Permissions** (`canGenerateAccomplishmentsReport`, `canViewAccomplishmentsReport`, `isAccomplishmentsReportOwner`, `canEditAccomplishmentsReportEntries`, `canCommentOnAccomplishmentsReport` in `permissions.ts`): `canGenerateAccomplishmentsReport` is trivial for person kind (always allowed — it's always about yourself) and reuses `canAccessCompany` for client kind. `canViewAccomplishmentsReport` is the real gate (see "Role-scoped view structure" above) — every provider method funnels through it. `canEditAccomplishmentsReportEntries` is the stricter owner-only subset used by `updateDraft`/`finalizeReport`. `canCommentOnAccomplishmentsReport` layers "not the owner, not an employee" on top of the view gate. On the client, the detail page doesn't re-derive any of this from `allUsers` — since `getReport` already returned a result, view access is already proven; the page only needs the self-contained `isAccomplishmentsReportOwner` plus the plain `isEmployee(user)` check to decide what to show.

**UI**: `/dashboard/reports` (role-scoped My Reports / Team Reports / All Reports sections, "Generate report" dialog: Person/Client toggle — no subject picker for Person, Today/This week/Custom range, a Trash link), `/dashboard/reports/[id]` (the editable view — read-only with a reviewer-comments card for supervisors/superadmins viewing someone else's report, editable with Save/Finalize/Delete for the owner), and `/dashboard/reports/trash` (Restore / confirmed permanent delete). ## Client Health Score (locked in Phase 3.1)

An at-a-glance, transparent health status per company — **On Track / Needs Attention / At Risk** — computed on every read from data already tracked, never stored and never a black-box number.

**The rule set lives in one pure function**, `computeClientHealth(workstreams, tasks, now?)` in `src/lib/data/client-health.ts` — plain data in, `{ status, reasons }` out, no provider/permission dependency of its own, so it's trivial to unit-test or retune later. Every threshold is a named constant at the top of that file:
- **Overdue tasks** — any open (`status !== "done"`) task whose `dueDate` is before today. **≥1 → at least Needs Attention; ≥3 → At Risk.**
- **Stalled workstreams** — an `active` workstream (on-hold/completed/cancelled workstreams are never "stalled," they're intentionally paused or done) whose most recent activity — the latest of its own `updatedAt` and its tasks' `updatedAt` — is **14+ days old**. **≥1 → at least Needs Attention; ≥2 → At Risk.**
- **Due-soon tasks** — open tasks due within the next 3 days. **≥3 with nothing else already wrong → Needs Attention** (a "heads up" signal, not a hard problem yet).
- **Overall progress and last activity** are always included as supporting context in the explanation, not scoring inputs by themselves.
- A company with **zero tasks at all** is trivially `on-track` ("No tasks tracked yet") rather than penalized for having no data.

**No hidden scoring**: `reasons` is a plain-English list built from the exact same facts the status came from — e.g. "3 overdue tasks · 0 stalled workstreams · 40% of tasks complete · last activity 9 days ago" — always includes overdue/stalled/progress/last-activity (even when zero, e.g. "0 overdue tasks"), plus a due-soon line only when there's at least one. `ClientHealthBadge` (compact, hover/focus tooltip showing the reasons — used in the companies list table) and `ClientHealthSummary` (badge + reasons always visible as plain text underneath, no hover needed — used prominently on the company detail page header) both read straight from `reasons.join(" · ")`, so list and detail pages can never drift out of sync with each other or with the underlying rule.

**Wiring**: `CompanyWithRelations` gained a `health: ClientHealth` field, computed inside `mockCompaniesProvider`'s existing `toCompanyWithRelations` by reading that company's own `db.workstreams`/`db.tasks` directly (the same "read `db` straight from a mock provider" pattern every derived field in this codebase already uses, e.g. `Workstream.progressPercent`). **Permissions were free**: `listCompanies`/`getCompany` already gate by the company-visibility rule (employee → assigned companies, supervisor → team's, superadmin → all) before `toCompanyWithRelations` ever runs, so health is just another derived field on an already-correctly-scoped response — no new permission function needed, and a viewer can never see health data for a company they can't otherwise access.

**Deliberate simplification**: "last activity" is resolved from `Task.updatedAt`/workstream `updatedAt` only — it does not look at checklist-item-level `completedAt` timestamps (that would need threading checklist items into the health computation's inputs too). A task whose only recent change was ticking a checklist item won't refresh its workstream's staleness clock. Noted here so it's an explicit, tunable trade-off, not a silent gap.

**Seed data**: three companies deliberately land in different tiers so the variety is visible without hunting — **Harborlight Insurance** (company-8) is On Track (added task-19, a recent, not-overdue task, so its otherwise-stale single completed task doesn't read as stalled), **Alderleaf Manufacturing** (company-1) is Needs Attention (1 overdue task, unchanged from existing data), and **Grovemont Realty** (company-7) is At Risk (added task-20, a third overdue task, on top of the two it already had).

**Not built this phase**: predictive/AI scoring, and any cross-client rollup view — this is per-company only (a client-level time-vs-budget rollup, a related but distinct signal, was added in Phase 3.18 — see "Time vs. Budget" below). "Stalled-client alerts" as a proactive notification (rather than a passive badge you have to look at) remains a distinct, unbuilt Phase 3 item.

## Saved Views (locked in Phase 3.2)

Personal, one-click filter shortcuts on top of the existing task search/filter system — save the current filter combination as a named view, apply it back instantly, rename or delete it later. Built on the exact same `TaskFilters` shape (`src/lib/data/hooks/use-task-filters.ts`) already shared by the Tasks list and My Day, so a saved view works identically on either screen with no per-screen adaptation.

**Data model**: `SavedView` (`src/lib/data/types/saved-view.ts`) is `{ id, userId, name, filters: SavedViewFilters, createdAt, updatedAt }`. `SavedViewFilters` mirrors `TaskFilters` field-for-field but is declared independently rather than imported from the hook module — a schema type in `types/` shouldn't depend on a `"use client"` hook file, so the two shapes are kept in sync by convention/comment rather than a shared import. No `scope` field distinguishing "saved from Tasks" vs. "saved from My Day" — a saved view is just a persisted filter combination, reusable wherever `TaskFilters`/`filterTasks` is already used.

**Provider** (`SavedViewsProvider` — `listSavedViews`/`createSavedView`/`renameSavedView`/`deleteSavedView`, mock + Supabase-stub, same swappable pattern as every other feature): every method scopes strictly to the requesting viewer's own views (`view.userId === viewer.id`, checked directly in each method) — no dedicated permission function needed, since "can see/rename/delete this view" reduces entirely to ownership. **Role/visibility for the underlying tasks needs no extra work at all**: applying a saved view only ever narrows a task list that `useTasks()`/`useMyTasks()` already fetched through the existing task-visibility gate, so a view can never surface a task the viewer wasn't already allowed to see.

**UI**: `SavedViewsBar` (`src/components/tasks/saved-views-bar.tsx`), rendered directly below `TaskFilterBar` on both `/dashboard/tasks` and `/dashboard/my-day`. Saved views show as pill-shaped chips (bookmark icon + name); clicking one calls the same `patch` function already passed to `TaskFilterBar`'s `onChange`, replacing the filter state wholesale in one call (safe because a saved view's `filters` object always has every field populated, never partial). The currently-active chip is highlighted (`secondary` variant) when the live filters exactly match a saved view's, mirroring the segmented-button active-state convention used elsewhere (e.g. List/Board toggles). Each chip has its own dropdown (Rename/Delete); Rename swaps the chip into an inline text input (Enter or the check button to confirm) rather than opening a dialog, matching the low-stakes, single-field nature of the edit. **Delete has no confirmation step** — a deliberate simplification given a saved view is personal, easily recreated, and never affects any real data; this is a lower blast-radius action than deleting a report or a company, which do confirm. "Save view" is disabled when the current filters are still just the defaults (`DEFAULT_TASK_FILTERS`) — nothing meaningful to name yet.

**Not built this phase**: sharing or team views (explicitly deferred, per the request — "personal" only), editing a saved view's filters after creation (only rename/delete; changing the filter combination means saving a new view), and any per-screen scoping of which views appear where (a view saved from either screen shows on both).

**Clear filters (small follow-up fix)**: a "Clear filters" button appears in `SavedViewsBar` whenever the live filters differ from `DEFAULT_TASK_FILTERS` — whether that's from typing/selecting a manual filter or from applying a saved view — and resets them wholesale back to the unfiltered list in one click. No extra state was needed to track "is a saved view active": since `DEFAULT_TASK_FILTERS` can never itself be saved as a view (the Save button is disabled at that state), clearing always falls back to filters no chip matches, so the active-chip highlight clears itself for free.

## Task List Grouping (locked in Phase 3.5)

A "Group by" control on the Tasks list view (`/dashboard/tasks`, List mode only) clusters the already-filtered task list under collapsible headers instead of one flat table — **Company, Activity, Workstream, Status, Assignee, or None** (the prior flat-list default). Deliberately orthogonal to filtering: filters narrow which tasks are present, grouping only organizes how the same set is displayed, so `filterTasks` was untouched.

**Data model**: `TaskGroupBy` (`src/lib/data/types/task.ts`) is a plain union type, imported by both the hooks layer and the schema layer — same "shared enum, independent shape" precedent `TaskStatus`/`TaskPriority` already set for `SavedViewFilters` vs. `TaskFilters`. `TaskFilters` and `SavedViewFilters` both gained a `groupBy: TaskGroupBy` field (default `"none"`), so **a saved view now remembers its group-by choice** exactly the same way it remembers its narrowing filters — no separate persistence path was needed, `groupBy` just rides along inside the same `filters` object every saved-view method already threads through. `SavedViewsBar`'s `filtersEqual` (active-chip highlight, "is this just the defaults" check) now compares `groupBy` too, so changing only the grouping is enough to enable "Save view," and a saved view only shows as active when its grouping matches the live one as well as its filters.

**Grouping logic** (`groupTasksBy`, `src/lib/data/hooks/use-task-filters.ts`): reads fields already present on `TaskWithRelations` — no new provider method or extra fetch. Company/Workstream group by `task.company`/`task.workstream` (id + name, already embedded per task); Activity groups by `task.activity` (department-qualified label, matching how the activity tag reads everywhere else) with an explicit "No activity tag" bucket for untagged work; Status groups in pipeline order (todo → in-progress → blocked → waiting-on-client → done), not alphabetically; Assignee groups by each of a task's assignees individually — **a multi-assignee task appears once per assignee**, since "group by assignee" reads most naturally as "show me everything Priya's on," not a single fragmented bucket per unique assignee-set. "No activity tag" and "Unassigned" buckets always sort last rather than wherever they'd alphabetically land; every other group sorts by label.

**UI**: `TaskGroupBySelect` (`src/components/tasks/task-group-by-select.tsx`) sits next to the List/Board toggle — not inside `TaskFilterBar` — since it's a display/organization control, not a filter, and (like the toggle) only makes sense in List mode; it's hidden entirely in Board view. Board itself is completely unchanged — it still groups by status into its own fixed drag-and-drop columns and was given zero new props or logic. Each group renders under a chevron + name + mono task count header; clicking it toggles that group's `aria-expanded` and shows/hides its contents — collapse state is local `Set<string>` component state, not persisted. **Ungrouped ("None") still renders as the original flat `<Table>`; grouped mode's row-vs-card presentation changed in Phase 3.6 — see below.**

**Role visibility**: no new permission function — grouping only reorganizes whatever `filterTasks` already narrowed down from `useTasks()`, which itself already enforces the existing task-visibility gate (employee/supervisor/superadmin) before this feature ever sees the list. The Tasks list page itself remains supervisor/superadmin-only, unchanged from Phase 1b.

**Not built this phase**: any grouping on My Day or the employee dashboard's task panel (scoped to "the tasks list view," per the request); persisting collapsed/expanded group state across reloads; nested/multi-level grouping (e.g. Company → Status).

## Page Transitions (locked in Phase 3.7)

A subtle fade + slight upward slide plays whenever any route under `/dashboard` changes, for every role — pure navigation polish, no page content or logic touched.

**Implementation**: `PageTransition` (`src/components/dashboard/page-transition.tsx`) wraps `{children}` inside `src/app/dashboard/layout.tsx`'s `<main>`, keyed by `usePathname()`. Changing the `key` forces React to remount the wrapper on every navigation — including between two instances of the *same* dynamic route template (e.g. one task detail to another), which wouldn't otherwise necessarily remount just from a route-param change — so the enter animation reliably replays on every navigation, not just top-level route changes. No page component's own state/logic was touched; a route change already unmounts/remounts genuinely different pages regardless of this feature, so the only behavioral effect is that same-template navigations (detail-to-detail) now also reset local UI-only state (e.g. a locally-open toggle), which is the same thing that already happens for every other route change and is the expected behavior when moving to a different record.

**Motion**: built entirely from `tw-animate-css` utilities already used elsewhere in the app for exactly this kind of enter/exit animation (the Dialog/Select/Sheet popups already use `animate-in`/`fade-in-0`/`zoom-in-95`/`slide-in-from-*`) — no new keyframes, no new dependency. Deliberately uses a plain `ease-out` rather than the app's springy `ease-spring` (used for hover-lift elsewhere): the request explicitly wanted calm polish, not a bounce, so a different easing was the correct choice even though it's the "odd one out" relative to hover-lift. `duration-300` matches the duration scale several other dashboard hover/chart transitions already use.

**Reduced motion**: no special-case code was needed. `globals.css` already has a blanket `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` rule forcing `animation-duration: 0.01ms !important` on every element — added during the dashboard-redesign audit specifically because the original Plexa reference's scroll-reveal effect had no such guard. Verified via Playwright's `reducedMotion: "reduce"` emulation that the computed `animation-duration` on the transition wrapper collapses from `0.3s` to `1e-05s` automatically, with zero additional logic in `PageTransition` itself.

**Not built this phase**: no per-route custom transition variants (every route gets the same fade + slide); no exit animation (only enter — Next.js App Router doesn't have an easy "old page still mounted while new page enters" primitive without a routing library, so this stays enter-only, consistent with "keep it quick and gentle" rather than adding complexity for a subtle exit effect); the `/login` route (outside `/dashboard`) is unaffected, since the request's examples (dashboard, tasks, companies, reports, my-day) are all post-login navigations.

## Content Stagger (locked in Phase 3.8)

On top of the Phase 3.7 page-level fade/slide, each page's own content now cascades in — KPI cards, section panels, task/company/report rows, and dashboard list items each get a small additional entrance delay so they appear in a gentle wave instead of all at once.

**Implementation**: a single shared primitive, `src/lib/stagger.ts` — `STAGGER_ITEM_CLASS` (the same `fade-in-0`/`slide-in-from-bottom-1`/`ease-out` shape as the Phase 3.7 page transition, plus `fill-mode-backwards` so an item stays invisible during its delay instead of flashing at full opacity first) and `staggerDelay(index)`, which returns an inline `animationDelay` of `index * 80ms`, capped at index 4 (5 items with a distinct delay: 0/80/160/240/320ms) so long lists/grids settle instead of trailing out. Applied at every `.map()` call site across all three dashboards' KPI-card rows and section panels, the Tasks/Companies/Reports list and grid views (including `TaskRowList` and `TaskGridCard`, so every consumer of those shared components gets it for free), and the dashboard summary cards (Recent Notifications, Upcoming Deadlines, Team Activity, Team Workload, Reports Awaiting Review, Brand Snapshot, Client Health at-risk list). `StatCard`, `WorkstreamOverviewCard`, `TaskGridCard`, and `ClientHealthOverviewCard` each gained optional `className`/`style` passthrough to support this — no other prop surface changed.

**Tuning pass**: the first cut (`duration-300`, a 40ms step) read as too fast to actually perceive as a cascade — items landed close enough together that the reveal looked instantaneous. Bumped the per-item duration to `duration-500` (500ms, still `ease-out`) so each element's own fade/rise is clearly visible, and the per-item step to 80ms so the cascade between items reads as a graceful one-after-another wave rather than a simultaneous flash. Verified via Playwright that the worst-case cascade (last capped item's delay + its own duration) tops out at 820ms on every page across all three roles — comfortably under the ~1s "shouldn't feel like it's making the user wait" budget even on the densest dashboards.

**Reduced motion**: the existing blanket `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` rule in `globals.css` (added in the Phase 3.4 audit) only forced `animation-duration`/`transition-duration` to near-zero; it didn't touch `animation-delay`, which would have left staggered items popping in one-by-one on a near-zero timer instead of collapsing to a single instant reveal. Added `animation-delay: 0ms !important` to the same rule to close that gap — verified via Playwright's `reducedMotion: "reduce"` emulation that every staggered item's computed delay collapses to `0s` and duration to `1e-05s`.

**Not built this phase**: the Kanban board view (`TaskBoard`) is untouched, consistent with "Board view stays as-is" from Phase 3.6; detail pages (task/company/workstream/report single-record views) are out of scope — the request's examples were all list/dashboard pages, so detail-page motion wasn't touched.

## Keyboard Shortcuts (Phase 3.11 — app-wide, all three roles)

Tasteful, discoverable power-user navigation — entirely a convenience layer over things that were already clickable; no new capability, no new dialog/route that didn't already exist.

**`KeyboardShortcuts`** (`src/components/dashboard/keyboard-shortcuts.tsx`), mounted once in `src/app/dashboard/layout.tsx` (so it's live on every authenticated page, same as `PageTransition`), attaches a single global `keydown` listener:
- **`g` then a letter** navigates — `g d` Dashboard, `g m` My Day, `g c` Companies, `g t` Tasks, `g r` Reports (`router.push`, no page reload). The leading `g` is "pending" for 1.2s waiting for its second key, then cancels itself — press `g` then anything else (or nothing) and it's a no-op, same as GitHub's own `g`-prefixed scheme this mirrors.
- **`/`** focuses this page's search field, **`n`** clicks this page's "new task" button, if either exists here — via a plain `data-shortcut="search"` / `data-shortcut="new-task"` attribute added to the already-existing element (`TaskFilterBar`'s search `Input`, reused everywhere it already renders; `Input` on `/dashboard/companies`; the "Add task"/"New Task" `Button` on the Employee dashboard, `/dashboard/tasks`, all three My Day views, and the company/workstream detail pages' Tasks cards) — `document.querySelector` simply finds nothing on a page without one, so this is a no-op there, not an error path. No new task-creation logic anywhere; whichever dialog that button already opens is exactly what opens.
- **`1`–`5`** switch My Day's status buckets, only when `pathname === "/dashboard/my-day"`. `StatusBucketButton` (`src/components/my-day/status-bucket-button.tsx`) computes its own `data-shortcut="bucket-N"` from `STATUS_ORDER.indexOf(status) + 1` — no prop threading needed through any of the three My Day components to wire this up.
- **`?`** opens `ShortcutsHelpDialog` — a plain `Dialog` (same primitive every other modal in the app already uses) listing everything above, grouped **Navigation / Actions / On My Day**, each row a label plus `<kbd>` chips styled with the app's existing mono micro-label convention.

**Role visibility**: `NAV_SHORTCUTS` (`src/lib/keyboard-shortcuts.ts`) is the single source of truth for the nav chords, each entry carrying an optional `isVisible(user)` gate — Tasks uses `canManageTasks`, the exact same function `AppSidebar` already gates its own "Tasks" nav item with, so the shortcut list can never drift from what the sidebar itself offers. `ShortcutsHelpDialog` filters `NAV_SHORTCUTS` through the same gate before rendering, so an Employee's help overlay simply never lists `g t` — not shown as disabled, not shown at all. Verified: `g t` is a no-op for Employee, navigates normally for Supervisor/Superadmin, and the help overlay's Tasks row is present/absent to match.

**Never hijacks typing**: `isTypingTarget` checks `event.target` for `INPUT`/`TEXTAREA`/`SELECT`/`isContentEditable` and bails immediately — verified by typing the literal characters "g" and "d" into a focused search field and confirming they land as text, not a navigation. A second guard, `isOverlayOpen`, checks for any open `Dialog`/`Select` popup (`[data-slot="dialog-content"], [data-slot="popup"]` — Base UI only renders these while open) and suppresses every shortcut while true, so shortcuts never fire behind or into an already-open modal (e.g. `g d` while the new-task dialog is open does nothing, confirmed). Both guards run before any chord/action logic, and a third check skips modifier-held presses (`ctrlKey`/`metaKey`/`altKey`) and key-repeat events, so OS/browser shortcuts and held-key auto-repeat are both left alone. `Shift` is deliberately not blocked — `?` requires it on a US keyboard layout, and matching on `event.key` (the produced character) rather than the physical key handles this correctly for free.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles: every nav chord (including the role-gated `g t`), `/`-focus, `n`-opens-dialog on both My Day and the Tasks page, `1`–`5` bucket switching, the help overlay's role-filtered content, the "don't hijack typing" guard (literal "gd" typed into a focused field), the "suppressed while a dialog is open" guard, and the chord's 1.2s expiry (a late second keypress after the pending window closes does not navigate). Confirmed in both light and dark mode — `ShortcutsHelpDialog` uses the same `Dialog`/`kbd`-chip styling as the rest of the design system with no new colors or tokens.

**Not built this phase**: no visual "g…" pending-chord indicator (kept invisible/silent, matching "subtle" — a stray `g` with no follow-up is simply inert); no shortcut customization/remapping; no shortcuts for actions that don't already have an existing single button to reuse (e.g. no dedicated "new company" key, since the request was specifically about task creation and navigation); no mobile/touch equivalent (this is a keyboard-only, desktop power-user feature by nature).

## Font Consistency Audit (Phase 3.12 — app-wide, all three roles)

**Two real, fixable inconsistencies were found and fixed** — both genuine drift from the app's own established conventions, not from the reference:

1. **17 "mono micro-label" spans were missing `font-mono`.** Every uppercase, tracked, muted micro-label in the app (`StatCard`'s label, `SectionBreak`, every `TableHead` across Tasks/Companies/Reports/Trash, the running-timer caption, donut captions, etc. — 40+ instances) shares one exact recipe: `font-mono text-xs tracking-wide(r) text-muted-foreground uppercase`. 2. **Heading-level/display-value elements weren't consistently wearing the `font-heading` token**, even though the token already exists (`--font-heading: var(--font-sans)` in `globals.css`) and `CardTitle`/`DialogTitle`/`SheetTitle` already used it. Every page `<h1>` (list/detail page titles, both My Day and dashboard hero headings), `StatCard`'s big value, the My Day status-bucket counts, the "Today" time totals, both donuts' center totals, and `AvatarFallback`'s initials were all just inheriting `font-sans` directly instead of going through `font-heading`. **This currently renders identically** — `--font-heading` and `--font-sans` both resolve to Inter today — so nothing *looks* different yet, but it closes a latent inconsistency: before this fix, only three of the app's ~20 heading/display-value element types would have picked up a future change to `--font-heading` (e.g. a real display typeface), and the rest would have silently stayed Inter. Fixed by adding `font-heading` to all of the above, matching the same token every card/dialog/sheet title already uses.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles (Employee/Supervisor/Superadmin), in both light and dark mode: `getComputedStyle(...).fontFamily` confirmed `body`/`button`/page `<h1>`s/`AvatarFallback` resolve to Inter, and mono micro-labels (including the newly-fixed detail-page field labels) resolve to DM Mono, on the dashboards, My Day, Tasks/Companies/Workstreams/Reports list and detail pages. `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npm run lint`, and `npm run build` all clean.

**Not built this phase**: no new font, no new type scale, no change to font *sizes* or *weights* anywhere (the `report-view.tsx` weight change above was necessary to avoid a synthetic-bold render at an unsupported DM Mono weight, not a stylistic choice); `--font-heading` still equals `--font-sans` — actually loading a distinct display typeface (matching the reference's unloaded `'Artific'` fallback-to-Inter) is a possible future decision, not part of this audit.

## Command Palette (Phase 3.13 — app-wide, all three roles)

**Filtering and browsing**: a category-tab row (All / Companies / Tasks / Workstreams / Reports / People / Actions — tabs only appear for categories that are non-empty for the viewer's role) narrows the results; typing does a dead-simple case-insensitive substring match against each result's title/subtitle (same "no fuzzy matching, no ranking model" precedent as the Activity Catalog's keyword suggestion). The highlight resets to the top when the query or tab changes (adjusted during render, per React's own guidance, not in an effect) and is clamped at read time against the live result count rather than synced back into a second piece of state, so a background data fetch finishing mid-session can never leave it pointing past the end of the list.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles, in both light and dark mode: palette opens via topbar click and via Ctrl+K (including while a normal page search field is focused, without disturbing normal typing — confirmed by typing the literal characters "gk" into a field and seeing them land as text); People tab is absent for Employee and present for Supervisor/Superadmin (excluding the viewer); arrow-key + Enter navigation lands on the correct company/task/report/workstream detail page; browsing a category tab with an empty query lists that category's full data; the "New task" action closes the palette and opens the real task-form dialog; a second Ctrl+K press while already open doesn't stack a second dialog; `getComputedStyle` confirms Inter rendering and the backdrop's blur/rounded-corner styling in both themes.

### Completion animation fix (all three roles)

**Root cause**: marking done the *last remaining task in a bucket* silently killed the animation. `BucketTaskGrid` owns local `exiting` state for the card mid-animation, but the three My Day pages decided whether to render `<BucketTaskGrid>` **at all** based on `bucketTasks.length === 0` — and the instant the refetch after `updateTaskStatus` resolves, that just-completed task no longer matches the selected bucket, so `bucketTasks` becomes empty and the parent swaps to its empty-state message. That **unmounts `BucketTaskGrid` mid-exit-animation**, discarding its `exiting` snapshot before the CSS animation ever gets to play — the card just vanished instantly. This only reproduced with the bucket's *last* task (2+ remaining tasks meant `bucketTasks` stayed non-empty, keeping the grid mounted, which is why it looked like it "worked sometimes"). **Fix**: `BucketTaskGrid` now takes an `emptyMessage` prop and decides its own empty-vs-grid branching internally, based on `combined.length` (bucket tasks *plus* anything still mid-exit-animation) instead of the caller's raw `tasks.length` — so the grid never unmounts out from under a card that's still animating, regardless of whether it was the bucket's last task. All three My Day pages now render `<BucketTaskGrid ... emptyMessage={...} />` unconditionally instead of branching before it.

**More visible, still quick**: `TaskGridCard`'s checkbox now swaps to a `CheckCircle2` icon in `text-success` (green) the instant it's checked, replacing the shared `Checkbox` component's own generic primary-colored fill for this one moment — a deliberate, scoped choice (overriding the shared `Checkbox`'s global checked-color would have recolored every checkbox in the app, e.g. `TaskChecklist`, `ReusePastTaskDialog`'s "Also copy description"). Same `animate-in zoom-in-50 duration-200 ease-spring` pop as before, just visibly green instead of accent-colored. The card's exit animation gained `zoom-out-95` alongside its existing `fade-out-0 slide-out-to-bottom-2` — a slight scale-down layered onto the fade+slide, reading as more deliberate ("not a subtle fade") without lengthening the total ~520ms (220ms pop + 300ms exit) sequence.

**How to test it**: go to My Day (any role), select a bucket with at least one open task assigned to you, and click its checkbox. You should see, in order: the checkbox itself instantly swap to a **green** checkmark that pops in (~200ms), then — starting at roughly the 220ms mark — the whole card fades, scales down slightly, and slides down and out (~300ms), and only then does it disappear and the bucket's count update. This now reproduces identically whether it's the only task in the bucket or one of several — that specific case (the bucket's last task) was the one that silently broke before this fix.

**Reduced motion**: no new handling needed — both the checkmark pop and the card's exit animation go through the same existing global `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` CSS override every other animation in the app already respects; verified via Playwright's `reducedMotion: "reduce"` emulation that both collapse to a `1e-05s` computed `animationDuration` while the task still ends up marked done and the card is still removed, just without the visible motion.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles, in both light and dark mode: the attention strip's chip counts, expand/collapse, at-risk-only-for-Superadmin scoping, and Employee correctly having no strip at all; the completion animation now reliably plays through (green checkmark → scaled fade-slide exit → count decrement) confirmed specifically on a bucket's *last* task (the exact case that was broken) across all three roles; reduced motion collapses both animation durations while the completion still functions end-to-end.

**Not built this phase**: no change to the underlying attention signals, their permissions, or who can see what (Supervisor: team-scoped staff+reports; Superadmin: org-wide staff+reports+at-risk clients — both unchanged); no persisted "which category was last expanded" (resets to collapsed on every page load); no undo/toast on task completion (unchanged from the original Delight Pass decision — the animation itself is still the only feedback); no change to `TaskBoard`/Kanban or the Tasks list's own `TaskGridCard` grid, which still never passes `onMarkDone` and so shows no checkbox either way.

## Time vs. Budget (Phase 3.18 — all three roles; field renamed/re-based to minutes in Phase 3.31; module genericized + reused per-task in Phase 3.32; workstream side now a roll-up from tasks in Phase 3.34)

A transparent, no-hidden-scoring view comparing each workstream's already-tracked expected-time budget against its actual logged hours — resolving the deferral both Client Health Score (Phase 3.1) and Team Workload explicitly called out ("any time-vs-expected-hours signal, deferred until the workstream's own estimate is actually used for anything"). **As of Phase 3.31**, the underlying field is `Workstream.expectedMinutes` (minutes, not hours) and `WorkstreamBudget`/`WorkstreamHours` were renamed to match — the rule set and thresholds described below are otherwise unchanged. **As of Phase 3.32**, the module itself moved and was renamed again — `src/lib/data/workstream-budget.ts` → `src/lib/data/time-budget.ts` (`WorkstreamBudget`→`TimeBudget`, `WorkstreamHours`→`TimeBudgetInput`) and `src/components/workstreams/budget-bar.tsx` → `src/components/ui/budget-bar.tsx` (`WorkstreamBudgetBar`→`BudgetBar`, `WorkstreamBudgetBarCompact`→`BudgetBarCompact`) — because the same domain-generic logic is now also reused for the per-task estimate-vs-actual view (see "Time Tracking Overhaul" below); `computeWorkstreamBudget`/`computeBudgetRollup` kept their original names.

## Recurring Work (Phase 3.19 — all three roles)

Workstreams (and templates) can now be configured with a **real** cadence — frequency + anchor date — replacing the opaque `recurrenceRule` string label both entities carried since Phase 2.0/2.1 ("no recurrence engine yet"). A manual **"Generate next occurrence"** action clones the current workstream's own tasks into a new period, with due dates shifted and expected hours carried over, previewed before it commits — the same "form → preview → commit" shape as "Apply template."

**Be honest about automation, per the request**: everything here is **manual, user-triggered generation** — there is no background scheduler, cron job, or timer of any kind. "Due now" is a plain computed fact (today ≥ the next occurrence date), never an automatic trigger. Actual scheduled auto-generation needs a real backend to run on a clock the mock provider has no equivalent of — that's Phase 4, and is called out explicitly below rather than faked with, say, a `setInterval` that would silently stop working the moment the tab closes.

**Data model** — `RecurrenceFrequency` (`weekly | monthly | quarterly | yearly | custom`, `src/lib/data/types/recurrence.ts`) replaces the old free-text label everywhere. `Workstream` gained `recurrenceFrequency` (null = not recurring), `recurrenceAnchorDate` (the fixed reference date the cadence steps from — never moves, even across generations, so the schedule can't drift), `recurrenceCustomIntervalDays` (only meaningful for `custom`), and `previousOccurrenceWorkstreamId` (set only by "Generate next occurrence," pointing at the workstream this one continues from). `Template` gained the same `recurrenceFrequency`/`recurrenceCustomIntervalDays` pair in place of its old label — still seed-only, like every other Template field (Phase 2.1's "no in-app template editor yet" is unchanged); "Apply template" now carries a template's real cadence onto the created workstream (anchored to the chosen start date) instead of copying a cosmetic string.

**The rule set lives in one pure module**, `src/lib/data/recurrence.ts` — `computeNextOccurrenceDate(config, afterDate)` steps forward from the anchor by whole cadence intervals until it finds the first date strictly after `afterDate`, so a workstream's "next" is always aligned to the fixed anchor rather than wherever this particular instance happened to start (handles month-length clamping, e.g. Jan 31 + 1 month → Feb 28/29, not March 3). Returns `null` only when a `custom` cadence has no interval day-count set yet — an honest "can't compute this" rather than a silent guess. `computeWorkstreamRecurrence(config, startDate, hasSuccessor, today)` bundles everything a screen needs — `isActive` (false once a *later* workstream has been generated from this one — no chain-tracking needed beyond a plain `db.workstreams.some(w => w.previousOccurrenceWorkstreamId === this.id)` lookup), `nextOccurrenceDate`, and `isDue` — into one computed, no-hidden-scoring object, same shape as `WorkstreamBudget`/`ClientHealth`.

**Wiring**: `WorkstreamWithRelations` gained a `recurrence: WorkstreamRecurrenceInfo | null` field, computed inside `mockWorkstreamsProvider`'s existing `toWorkstreamWithRelations` — **permissions were free** again, since `listWorkstreams`/`getWorkstream` already gate by workstream visibility before this ever runs. No new provider method exists anywhere in this phase (mirroring "Apply template" and Time vs. Budget's precedent): "Generate next occurrence" is pure client-side orchestration — fetch the source workstream's current tasks (already on hand via the detail page's own `useTasks`), call `workstreamsProvider.createWorkstream` once with the same cadence fields plus `previousOccurrenceWorkstreamId` set, then `tasksProvider.createTask` once per cloned task with its due date shifted by the same day-offset from the new start date, checklist items copied fresh and unchecked, and assignees carried over as-is (or left genuinely unassigned via `allowUnassigned` if the source task had none) — status always resets to `todo` regardless of what the source task's current status was.

**A workstream never nags twice**: once "Generate next occurrence" runs, the source workstream's `isActive` flips to false (a later one now exists in the chain) and its own "Generate" button disappears — the new occurrence carries the live indicator forward instead. Generating is **not gated on being due** — a supervisor can generate ahead of schedule if they want to get a jump on next period; "due now" only drives the passive indicators and the dashboard round-up below, never blocks or forces the action.

**Indicators, everywhere a workstream already shows**: `RecurrenceIndicator` (prominent — a cadence badge plus the plain-English next-occurrence fact, amber when due) sits on the workstream detail page next to the status/brand badges; `RecurrenceIndicatorCompact` (a one-line "↻ Recurs monthly · due now" caption) sits under the budget bar on `WorkstreamOverviewCard` (dashboard "My Workstreams") and the company detail page's Workstreams rows — both `src/components/workstreams/recurrence-indicator.tsx`, rendered only when `recurrence` is non-null, so non-recurring workstreams show nothing extra at all.

**"List any due now"**: a new `RecurringWorkDueCard` (`src/components/dashboard/recurring-work-due-card.tsx`) on the Supervisor and Superadmin dashboards' second section — same capped-list, link-out pattern as `ReportsAwaitingReviewCard`, fed straight from each dashboard's own already-role-scoped `useWorkstreams()`. Not added to the Employee dashboard (employees can't configure recurrence or generate) or as a separate company-detail card (the per-row compact indicator already covers that scope; a second near-duplicate list there would be redundant).

**Seed data**: five Jan/2026-started monthly workstreams (Accounting 2026, both Payroll 2026s, Bookkeeping 2026, the Digital Marketing 2026 under Croki Digital) are due now — a realistic multi-month backlog, since none has ever had "Generate" clicked. IT Support Discovery was additionally given a monthly cadence anchored to its own July start specifically to demonstrate the **not-yet-due** state (`next: Aug 17, 2026`) without hunting for one. Generating from a due workstream only advances by one period at a time, by design — even a 6-month backlog resolves one click at a time, matching the "next occurrence," singular, framing of the action.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles, light and dark mode: due/not-due/superseded states all compute correctly (confirmed the superseded transition specifically via in-app client-side navigation, not a hard reload — the mock provider's in-memory `db` resets on a real page load, same as every other mock-backed feature in this app); the full generate → preview → confirm flow correctly shifts due dates and carries expected hours/assignees; Supervisor/Superadmin see the dashboard due-list scoped to their own visibility, Employee sees neither that card nor any Edit/Generate control (view-only recurrence indicators only); "Apply template" still applies a recurring template's cadence correctly onto the new workstream. **A real bug was caught and fixed during verification**: the preview step's task-count sentence ("4 tasks will be created...") silently dropped the space between "tasks" and "will" — the same JSX text/expression-adjacency footgun fixed in Phase 3.9's My Day heading — fixed by collapsing the whole sentence into one template-string expression instead of mixed JSX children.

**A real bug was caught and fixed during verification**: toggling the new Recurrence `Switch` on, then submitting via **Cmd/Ctrl+Enter without an intervening click elsewhere**, silently dropped the recurrence setting — the created workstream came back non-recurring even though the switch was visibly on right before submitting. Root cause: the switch retains DOM focus after being toggled (a native `<label>`-click forwards to its nested control), and Base UI's `Switch` treats a bare `Enter` as its own activation key (flipping `checked` back off) — a live behavior of the component (confirmed independently: pressing plain `Enter` while it's focused toggles it with no `Ctrl` involved at all), not a default browser action. Fixed by moving the listener to the **capture phase** and adding `stopPropagation()`, so it intercepts Cmd/Ctrl+Enter before the event ever reaches whatever control currently has focus. ## Daily Update — Phase A (Phase 3.23 — data model, auto-draft, "fill your own" on My Day)

A per-person, per-day log of tracked work — Date · Client · Activity · Time · Progress · Details — auto-drafted from that day's tasks/handoffs, then freely edited before confirming. Smaller, daily-cadence sibling of the Accomplishments Report (see above), and the intended future evidence source for a weekly-report redesign (noted below, not built yet). **This is Phase A**: data model, auto-draft engine, permissions, and "fill your own" on My Day, shipped first per an explicit "two reviewable phases" choice. **Phase B — the "Team Updates" browsing page — shipped as its own follow-up; see "Team Updates" below.**

**Data model**: `DailyUpdate` (`src/lib/data/types/daily-update.ts`) is one stored-snapshot document per `(userId, date)` — `status: "draft" | "confirmed"`, a flat `entries: DailyUpdateEntry[]` (not a department/brand tree like the Accomplishments Report, since Date · Client · Activity · Time · Progress · Details is inherently one row per event). Each `DailyUpdateEntry` carries `source: "task" | "handoff-sent" | "handoff-received" | "manual"`, `sourceTaskId`/`sourceHandoffId` (the provenance and the live-merge key, see below — both null on a manual entry, which has no backing task/handoff), snapshotted `companyLabel`/`activityLabel`, `minutesLogged`, `progressStatus` (the task's real `TaskStatus`, reusing `TaskStatusBadge`'s existing color mapping — no new status vocabulary; `null` for handoff and manual entries, which instead get a plain `progressLabel` like "Handed off to Priya Nair" or "Manual entry") and `details` — the one field the person edits on an auto-drafted entry (every field is person-entered up front on a manual one), everything else is a computed fact. Deliberately simpler than the Accomplishments Report: no `history` log, no comments — reopening just flips `status` back to `"draft"` with no audit trail.

**Provider** (`DailyUpdatesProvider` — `getMyTodayUpdate`/`listUpdatesForDate`/`updateEntryDetails`/`addManualEntry`/`confirmUpdate`/`reopenUpdate`, mock + Supabase-stub, same swappable pattern as every other feature): six methods, not eleven — "generate" and "fetch" collapse into one `getMyTodayUpdate` call since today's update is always exactly one record. `listUpdatesForDate` is a pure read that never creates a placeholder for someone who hasn't opened My Day yet, powering Team Updates' "Not started / Draft / Confirmed" roster distinction from one call.

**Auto-draft + live-merge engine** (the one genuinely new algorithm, in `mock-daily-updates-provider.ts`): `computeFreshEntries(userId, date)` scans `db.tasks` for anything the person logged time on or changed status on that day (`task.statusChangedById === userId && task.statusChangedAt` matching the date — a task can qualify from status change alone, with zero time logged), plus `db.taskHandoffs` created that day where they're either the sender or the recipient — extending the Accomplishments Report's own `gatherEvidence` precedent with the one source it doesn't cover (handoffs), and reshaping "aggregate into department buckets" into "one entry per touched thing" (a task both received *and* worked on the same day produces two separate entries, not a merged one, so the handoff is never hidden). `getMyTodayUpdate` re-runs this scan on every call while `status === "draft"`, and `mergeEntries` matches fresh entries against prior ones by a stable composite key (`source:sourceTaskId:sourceHandoffId`) — preserving the person's already-typed `details` text and the entry's `id`, while refreshing everything else (minutes/status may have moved on). A confirmed update is returned untouched, no re-scan. Auto-draft is never forced or auto-submitted — the person owns the final text, and an empty draft (nothing tracked yet) is created just from opening My Day, intentionally, so a future roster view can tell "opened My Day, nothing to log" apart from "never opened My Day."

**Permissions** (`permissions.ts`, direct structural mirror of the Accomplishments Report set): `isDailyUpdateOwner`, `canViewDailyUpdate` (owner always; employees see only their own, full stop; supervisor sees direct reports; superadmin sees everyone — same shape as `canViewAccomplishmentsReport`), `canEditDailyUpdate`/`canReopenDailyUpdate` (owner-only, gated by `status`), and `canViewTeamUpdatesPage` (gates the Team Updates page to supervisor/superadmin, since an employee has no one "below" them to browse). Every provider method re-checks the relevant function itself, not just the UI — same "every provider method enforces its own gate" convention as every other feature in this app. `addManualEntry` reuses `canEditDailyUpdate` as-is (owner-only, draft-only) — adding a manual entry is just another edit to the day's draft, not a new permission axis.

**Reopen/edit a confirmed update, never permanently locked**: `canReopenDailyUpdate` (owner-only, confirmed-only) already covered this from the start — a confirmed day flips back to `"draft"` via `reopenUpdate`, at which point `getMyTodayUpdate`'s live-merge resumes and `canEditDailyUpdate` allows editing/adding entries again; re-confirming freezes it again. **Only the owner** can reopen or edit — a supervisor/superadmin can *view* an update they manage on Team Updates (per `canViewDailyUpdate`) but every mutating method (`updateEntryDetails`/`addManualEntry`/`confirmUpdate`/`reopenUpdate`) rejects anyone but the owner, and an unrelated employee can't even view it in the first place. Team Updates itself never offers an edit control at all (see below), so this was also confirmed directly at the provider level: a supervisor's attempt to add/edit/reopen an employee's update throws; the owner's own confirm → reopen → edit → re-confirm cycle succeeds end to end.

**Manual entries — auto-draft plus manual, not auto-only**: `addManualEntry(viewer, updateId, { companyId, activityId, minutesLogged, details })` lets the person log something the auto-draft never sees — a meeting, a call, "Other" — with the same shape as an auto-drafted entry, except **client and activity are both optional** (an auto-drafted entry always has a real company; a manual one may have neither). The "Add entry" affordance (`AddManualEntryDialog`, `src/components/daily-updates/add-manual-entry-dialog.tsx`) is always available on a draft, not just on an empty day — reuses `useCompanies()` for the client picker and the existing brand-scoped `useActivityCatalog(brand.id)` for the activity picker (same "pick a company, activity narrows to its brand's catalog" pattern the Task form already established), both fully optional so submitting with neither selected is valid. A manual entry's `entryKey` for the live-merge is keyed by its own `id` rather than a task/handoff id (it has none) — harmless in practice since `computeFreshEntries` never produces a `"manual"` entry to collide with, so a manual entry always lands in the merge's "still missing from fresh" bucket and is preserved exactly as entered, forever (until the person edits or the update is otherwise reopened).

**Graceful empty-day state**: when a draft has zero entries, `DailyUpdateEntries` now shows "No tracked work today — add an entry?" with an inline "Add entry" button, instead of the flatter "nothing tracked yet" message — but only when the view is editable and an add handler was actually supplied (Team Updates' read-only view, which passes neither, keeps the plainer "Nothing tracked yet today").

**My Day integration**: a shared `DailyUpdateCard` (`src/components/my-day/daily-update-card.tsx`, fed by `useMyTodayUpdate()`) sits in a "Daily Update" section on all three roles' My Day pages, between "Time & Deadlines" and "Activity." Status pill (Draft/Confirmed, mirroring `ReportStatusBadge`'s warning/success convention), an entry list (`daily-update-entries.tsx`, shared with the read-only Team Updates view via an `editable` prop) — each row a Client badge, Activity label, Time, Progress badge, and an autosave-on-blur `Textarea` for Details (no manual Save button — small blast radius per field, a deliberate simplification vs. the Accomplishments Report detail page's heavier draft/save machinery). A "Confirm" button locks it (toast: "Update confirmed"); a confirmed card shows "Confirmed at HH:MM" plus "Reopen," which flips it back to draft (editable Textareas and "Add entry" both reappear) so it can be corrected and re-confirmed.

**Weekly-report connection (noted, not built)**: once Daily Updates accumulate confirmed entries, a future weekly report's auto-draft could read a person's confirmed Daily Update entries for the week instead of re-deriving straight from raw tasks/time-entries — inheriting already-human-curated `details` text for free instead of regenerating it. No changes to the Accomplishments Report happen in this phase.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles, plus a direct provider-level check for the parts no UI can reach yet: a fresh My Day visit creates an empty draft showing the new "add an entry?" empty state; logging time on a task produces a task entry with the right company/activity/time/status; marking a task done with zero time logged also produces an entry (the status-change-only evidence path); creating a handoff produces a handoff-sent entry with sensible default Details; the live-merge behavior specifically — editing an entry's Details, then logging a second time entry on the same task, confirms the edited text survives unchanged while minutes update; adding a manual entry both with and without a client/activity renders correctly (a company on the one brand with a populated catalog correctly enables the activity picker; other companies correctly leave it disabled); the full confirm → reopen → edit → re-confirm cycle works and the edited text survives; "Add entry" and every Textarea correctly disappear once confirmed and reappear once reopened; a direct provider-level script (owner vs. their supervisor vs. an unrelated employee, `tsx` against the mock provider directly — this predates Team Updates existing, back when no UI path at all could reach anyone else's update) confirmed the supervisor's `addManualEntry`/`updateEntryDetails`/`reopenUpdate` calls are all rejected while the owner's own succeed, and the unrelated employee is rejected even earlier at the view gate; all three roles, light and dark mode.

**Not built this phase**: deleting or editing the client/activity/time on a manual entry after it's added (only its `details`, same as any entry, via the existing edit path — reopening and re-adding covers the rest); any use of Daily Update data by a weekly report (noted above only); any change to the Accomplishments Report.

**New hook**: `useDailyUpdatesForDate(date)` (`src/lib/data/hooks/use-daily-updates.ts`, alongside Phase A's `useMyTodayUpdate`) wraps `listUpdatesForDate`, re-fetching whenever `date` changes.

## Client Reports (Phase 3.25 — the client-facing weekly report redesign)

The headline "weekly report redesign" — a client-facing, name-free accomplishments report assembled from confirmed Daily Updates (falling back to raw task/time evidence for anyone who hasn't confirmed), department/activity sections that only appear when actually worked, and a layout matching the referenced ACAS "Insights — Saved Report" format (`design-reference/analysis-report__1__1.html`) using this app's own design tokens. Generates per client for a date range — daily, weekly, or custom, same three-value `rangeLabel` the internal report already uses.

**The one hard rule, stated up front**: no employee name may ever appear in the rendered or exported document — not in a section header, a detail line, a tooltip, the export filename, or the PDF's document title. Every structural field (department, activity, date, minutes) is an ID/enum/number by construction, so the *only* possible leak vector is the free-text `details` on a line item. That risk is accepted, not engineered away — the mitigation is the mandatory edit-before-finalize review (an explicit "make sure no employee names appear" reminder shown at the finalize confirmation step), not automated redaction. This was directly verified: a generated report's content sections and its full print/export view were checked against every seed user's full name (and each name's individual first/last fragments) and came back clean, and a reviewer comment containing ordinary text was confirmed to never reach the print view at all.

**Data model — a new parallel type, not a variant of Accomplishments Report**: `ClientReport` (`src/lib/data/types/client-report.ts`) is its own stored-snapshot document, deliberately independent of `AccomplishmentsReport` — the existing `kind: "client"` report is a same-shape, internal, fully-attributed variant (one row per activity, a single done+detail), which doesn't fit this report's needed shape (multiple dated, timed rows per activity) and was never actually built to be client-safe despite its name. Building a sibling type avoided any risk to that existing feature. Shape: `companyId`/`companyLabel`, `brandId`/`brandLabel`, a date range, `status: "draft" | "finalized"`, and `departments: ClientReportDepartmentSection[]` — each department holding `activities: ClientReportActivitySection[]`, each activity holding `lineItems: ClientReportLineItem[]` (`{ date, minutes, details, source }`). **Only departments/activities with actual line items ever appear** — there's no "every catalog activity, ticked or not" walk like the internal report; an unworked department simply isn't in the array. `comments`/`history`/`generatedByName` exist for internal record-keeping only (mirroring the internal report's reviewer-comment/integrity-log/attribution shapes exactly) and are explicitly documented as staff-screen-only, never printed.

**Auto-draft — per-person-per-day resolution, never double-sourced**: `computeDepartmentSections` (`mock-client-report-provider.ts`) walks every date in the range; for each date, finds every user who touched the company that day — either through one of its tasks (time logged or status changed) **or** through a confirmed Daily Update entry for that company that day (`contributorsForDate` checks both; the second check is load-bearing for manual Daily Update entries, which have no backing task and so were, until Phase 3.27's fix, invisible to the task/time-entry scan alone — see below); for each of those (day, person) pairs, resolves the line items exactly once — from that person's **confirmed** Daily Update entries for that company if one exists, otherwise from raw task/time evidence for just that person and that day. A person's own confirmed Daily Update is trusted as-is even if it happens to have nothing for this company that day (their reviewed record wins over unreviewed raw data) — only a genuinely missing or still-draft update falls back to raw. The raw-fallback path deliberately uses **only the task title + duration**, never task notes or time-entry notes — those are casual, never-reviewed internal remarks, a meaningfully higher leak risk than a person's own curated Daily Update prose. `formatMinutes`/`db.dailyUpdates`/`db.timeEntries`/`db.activities`/`db.departments` are all read directly, the same "mock providers freely read the shared `db` singleton" convention every other feature here uses.

**Only-worked-sections + "+ Add section"**: because departments/activities are only ever constructed from actual contributions, "only show what was worked" falls out of the generation algorithm for free — no filtering step, no dimmed-but-present placeholder rows like the ACAS reference itself uses for empty modules (a deliberate divergence from the literal ACAS pattern, since the request asked for full omission by default). `AddSectionDialog` (`src/components/client-reports/add-section-dialog.tsx`) lists the brand's full activity catalog minus whatever's already in the draft, letting the generator manually add an empty section to fill in by hand (each activity also gets an "+ Add line" for that); removing a section (or a line) is the exact opposite, single-step action, so a supervisor never has to fight the auto-draft to correct it. **Removing a line, not just a whole section, also cleans up after itself** (Phase 3.27): deleting an activity's last line item now drops the (now-empty) activity too, and the department along with it if that was its last activity — mirroring "remove section"'s own cleanup, so deleting the auto-draft's only entry for something never leaves an empty box behind by accident.

**Empty sections never reach a reader or the PDF (Phase 3.27 fix)**: a manually-added-but-not-yet-filled-in section is a real, valid state in the data — the owner might add it now and fill it in over several editing sessions — so it can't simply be forbidden from existing. What changed is where it's allowed to *render*: a new `visibleDepartments(departments, editable)` helper (`src/lib/data/client-report-totals.ts`) strips any activity with zero line items (and any department left with zero activities) whenever `editable` is false, and the detail page now feeds that filtered result — not the raw one — to the KPI band, the section-jump rail, and the report body alike, so all three always agree. The owner's own in-progress draft (`editable = true`) still renders the empty placeholder with its "Nothing added yet." row, so they know it's there to fill in or remove; a non-owner's read-only view, a finalized report (which is read-only even to its own owner), and that report's printed/exported PDF now never show one, regardless of how it got into the data — added on purpose and never filled in, or otherwise.

**Layout — mirrors ACAS's structural pattern, adapted to this domain**: an action header (colored initials tile, company name + status badge, `{brand} · {range}` subtitle, Export CSV/PDF buttons) → a 4-KPI band (Total Hours, Activities Covered, Days With Activity, Departments — real numbers only, no invented financial figures since this isn't a payroll report) → a two-column body: a sticky left "Report contents" rail (quick-jump to each department, no include/exclude toggle — unlike ACAS's fixed universe of ~13 possible modules, a section here either exists in the draft or doesn't, and add/remove already is the inclusion mechanism) and the department cards on the right, each a plain Details/Date/Duration table per activity with a department-total footer row, ending in one grand-total card. Built from this app's own tokens throughout (Inter/DM Mono, the light-blue/dark-mint primary already in `globals.css`), not new ones.

**Permissions** (`permissions.ts`, a direct structural mirror of the Accomplishments Report set): `canGenerateClientReport`/`canManageClientReports` require **supervisor or superadmin** — a deliberate difference from the internal report's "any employee, about their own work," since this is a formal document meant to leave the building. `canViewClientReport` additionally blocks employees outright (no "your own" client report an employee could ever own). `isClientReportOwner`/`canEditClientReportEntries`/`canReopenClientReport`/`canCommentOnClientReport` otherwise match the internal report's shapes exactly (owner-only edit/reopen while draft/finalized respectively; comment is supervisor/superadmin-non-owner only). Every provider method re-checks its own gate.

## "+ Add Service" — Manual Activities on the Internal Report (Phase 3.29, tightened to activity-level in Phase 3.30)

Brought the internal Accomplishments Report's department/activity inclusion in line with Client Reports' own "only what's real, plus a manual add" model, and gave it the same "+ Add service" affordance — closing the one behavioral gap between the two report types the ACAS redesign (Phase 3.28) had left unaddressed. The auto-draft's evidence-matching engine (`gatherEvidence`/`isTouched`/`buildLine` in `mock-accomplishments-report-provider.ts`) is completely unchanged throughout both phases; what changed is which activities the auto-draft's output *includes*, and a new manual way to include more.

**Before Phase 3.29**, a report's `departments[]` was always the brand's *entire* catalog (every department, every activity, ticked or not) — a deliberate "mirrors the paper form's full checklist" design locked back in Phase 2.2/2.3. **That default is superseded**: `buildBrandSections` now filters each department down to only its auto-detected activities (`.filter((a) => a.done)`), then drops any department left with zero activities — the same "only real work, by construction" posture Client Reports already had. **Phase 3.29 shipped this filter at the department level only** (an included department still listed its *entire* catalog, ticked or not) — real usage promptly surfaced the resulting bug: a genuinely-touched department (e.g. Accounting, because Reconciliation was done) still rendered every one of its *other* catalog activities as blank rows (Receipts, Payment requests, Check printing…), because nothing filtered *within* an already-included department. **Phase 3.30 tightened the filter to per-activity**, matching Client Reports' own granularity exactly — a department now only ever contains activities with real evidence, plus any added by hand. `other` (the untagged catch-all, a sibling of `departments[]`, not inside one) has a matching `otherIncluded: boolean` field on `AccomplishmentsReportBrandSection`, since it can't simply not exist in the array the way an excluded department/activity can — `otherIncluded` is what "is Other part of this report" means, for both auto-detection and manual add/remove.

**"+ Add service"** (`AddServiceDialog`, `src/components/reports/add-service-dialog.tsx`) is now the direct structural sibling of Client Reports' own `AddSectionDialog` — same search-a-catalog-grouped-by-department shape, same activity-level granularity, reusing `useActivityCatalog(brandId)` for the same Activity Catalog data both report types already draw from. Already-included activities (and Other, once included) are excluded from the picker.

No confirmation step, the same low-stakes, easily-undone posture Client Reports' own line/section removal already has.

**Final rule, via `visibleBrandSections(sections, editable)`** (`accomplishments-report-totals.ts`) — the direct structural sibling of Client Reports' `visibleDepartments`, also tightened to per-activity in Phase 3.30 for the same reason as generation: filtering only at the department level let a real, auto-detected activity's presence "vouch for" a separate empty one added by hand in the *same* department. ## Client Report Empty Rows + Staff-Name Safety Net (Phase 3.30)

Two independent fixes to the report surfaces, found via direct user testing of the live app (not caught by the ACAS redesign's own verification passes, since those exercised mostly seed/demo data rather than a report with several genuinely-touched activities in one department).

**Fix 1 — empty activity rows, covered in detail above**: the root issue was that "generated report shows only what actually has work" had only ever been enforced at the *department* level for the internal Accomplishments Report, never the *activity* level within an already-qualifying department. A department with one real touched activity still rendered every other activity in its full catalog, blank. Tightening `buildBrandSections`, `AddServiceDialog`, the detail page's add/remove handlers, `visibleBrandSections`, and the CSV export to per-activity granularity (detailed in "'+ Add Service'" above) closed this consistently across person and client-kind internal reports alike — Client Reports' own dedicated feature was already correct at this granularity from Phase 3.25 and needed no change.

**Fix 2 — staff-name safety net on Client Report finalize**: a client report's free-text `details` field is, by design, the one place a staff name could leak in (see "Client Reports" above — the mitigation has always been "mandatory edit-before-finalize review," never automated redaction, since a name can't be reliably told apart from an ordinary word by pattern-matching alone). This phase adds a best-effort, non-blocking *reminder* on top of that existing mitigation: `findMentionedStaffNames(departments, staff)` (new, `src/lib/data/client-report-name-scan.ts`) scans every line item's `details` text for a whole-word, case-insensitive match against each staff member's full name or any individual name fragment (first/middle/last; fragments under 3 characters are skipped to avoid noisy false positives like matching an initial). It's called with `useCompanyLookups().assignableStaff` — the same "staff the current viewer can already see" list already used elsewhere in this app (full org for a superadmin, self + direct reports for a supervisor), not a new all-users provider method.

The client report detail page (`/dashboard/reports/client/[id]/page.tsx`) runs this scan against the live draft the moment the owner clicks **Finalize** (entering the existing two-step confirm state), and — only if it finds something — shows an `Alert variant="warning"` right above the Cancel/Yes-finalize buttons: *"This client report mentions staff names: Alicia Chen. Client reports shouldn't name employees — please review before finalizing."* **This is a warning, never a block**: "Yes, finalize" stays fully clickable regardless of what the scan finds, exactly per the request — a manager can read the warning, go fix the text, or consciously proceed anyway (e.g. a false-positive match). Internal Accomplishments Reports (both person and client kind) are explicitly out of scope — those can legitimately contain staff names by design, so no scan runs there.

**Verified** via Playwright: generated a client report whose auto-drafted detail text (from a real seeded time-entry note) already read "...with Alicia..."; clicking Finalize showed the warning listing "Alicia Chen"; "Yes, finalize" proceeded anyway (not blocked) and the report finalized normally; reopened, edited the text to remove the name, saved, and clicking Finalize again showed no warning at all — confirming the scan re-runs against current content each time, not a stale/cached result.

## Task Quick Refinements — Status Contrast, Auto-Done, Expected Time Units, Explicit Client (Phase 3.31)

Four independent, unrelated fixes to Tasks/Workstreams, bundled since all four were requested together. No shared code between them except where noted.

**2. Checklist completion auto-sets status to Done.** `mockTasksProvider.toggleChecklistItem` now checks, after applying the toggle, whether every checklist item on the task is done: if so and the task isn't already `"done"`, it flips status to `"done"` (recording `statusChangedById`/`statusChangedAt`, same as a manual status change); if an item is later unticked on an already-`"done"` task, status reverts to **`"in-progress"`** — the chosen behavior, not `"todo"`, because work was clearly already happening on it and `"todo"` would misrepresent that. **3. Expected time — value + unit, normalized to minutes.** `Task.expectedHours`, `Workstream.expectedHours`, and `TemplateTask.expectedHours` (each a plain `number | null` of hours) are now `expectedMinutes` — the same nullable number, just minutes instead of hours, everywhere it's stored (types, provider interfaces, mock provider, seed data — existing hour values were multiplied by 60 to preserve their real meaning). A new `src/lib/data/expected-time.ts` defines the unit math: minutes/hours/days, where **a "day" is one 8-hour workday, not a 24-hour calendar day** (matching how this field was always meant as tracked work time, the same convention time-tracking tools like Harvest/Toggl use for capacity math) — `toMinutes`/`fromMinutes` convert, `bestUnitFor` picks the roundest-fitting unit for redisplaying an existing value (so a value entered as "2 days" redisplays as "2 days," not "2880 minutes"), and `formatExpectedTime` is the shared read-only formatter.
- New shared `ExpectedTimeInput` (`src/components/ui/expected-time-input.tsx`) — a number field + a minutes/hours/days `Select`, defaulting to minutes for a blank field per the request. Switching the unit **re-displays the same underlying duration in the new unit** rather than reinterpreting the typed number (a view change, not an accidental 60×/480× jump in the estimate) — e.g. "480" then switching to hours shows "8," not "480 hours." It owns its own local text/unit state (remounted via `key` on task/workstream id when the surrounding form resets, the same technique this app already uses elsewhere) rather than staying fully controlled, since re-deriving "what's currently being typed" from a plain minutes number on every keystroke would fight the input.
- Replaces the old plain "Expected hours" number field on both the task form (`TaskFormDialog`) and the workstream form (`WorkstreamFormDialog`) — the workstream form's Start/End/Expected-time row went from a cramped 3-column grid to 2 columns to give the value+unit control room.
- Every read-only display of this value — the task detail page, the workstream detail page, `TeamWorkloadCard`'s "Xh est." caption, the Apply Template preview's per-task and running-total figures, the Generate-next-occurrence preview's carried-over figures — now goes through `formatExpectedTime` instead of a hardcoded `${n}h` suffix.
- `workstream-budget.ts` (Time vs. Budget, Phase 3.18) was updated the same way: `WorkstreamHours`/`WorkstreamBudget.expectedHours` → `expectedMinutes`, and the budget-percent math now compares `actualMinutes`/`expectedMinutes` directly (a unit-invariant ratio) instead of converting to hours first. `budget-bar.tsx` still shows *actual* time in its own established `formatMinutes`-style hour label (unrelated to this feature, untouched) alongside the *expected* figure via `formatExpectedTime`.

**4. Explicit client on task creation.** A task's client was already knowable (derived from its workstream), but never shown up front. `TaskFormDialog`'s "Where it belongs" section gained a **Client** `Select` above the existing Workstream `Select` — picking a client narrows the workstream list to that client's own workstreams (via `useWorkstreams({ companyId })`'s existing optional filter, already supported, just not previously used here); picking a workstream directly (without pre-selecting a client) auto-syncs the Client select to that workstream's own company, so the two never disagree regardless of which one is picked first. A plain-text confirmation — *"Client: {company name}"* — sits directly under the Workstream select whenever one is selected, so the client is unmistakable even for someone who skips the Client select entirely and goes straight for the workstream. Prefilling from `defaultWorkstreamId` (opening the form from a workstream detail page) and edit mode (an existing task always knows its own company) both populate the Client select correctly with no extra clicks.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles, light and dark mode: status colors visibly distinguishable at a glance on the Tasks list, the Kanban board (columns and cards), My Day's buckets (selected vs. unselected), the task form's status pills (selected pill now a solid-looking colored chip, not a faint ring), and the status filter dropdown; created a task with two checklist items, ticked both — status auto-flipped to Done with a "Status last changed by" attribution — then unticked one — status correctly reverted to In progress; set an expected-time estimate as "2 days" on task creation and confirmed it round-tripped and displayed correctly on the detail page; picked a client first and confirmed the workstream list narrowed to just that client's workstreams, and confirmed the explicit "Client: {name}" line rendered; confirmed a supervisor and an employee both see the same Client picker and status colors on their own more limited task forms.

## Time Tracking Overhaul (Phase 3.32)

Adds Pause/Resume to task timers, fixes a real input bug, pairs the existing per-task/workstream time estimate with actual logged time, and gives supervisors/superadmins a Team Time view — mirroring Team Updates' shape. Deliberately kept to *logged-time only*: no geolocation/clock-in-out, no idle detection/screenshots/activity monitoring, no AI auto-generation. Every new label reads as "the person's own logged action," never as the system judging whether or how promptly someone paused.

**1. Pause / Resume — data model.** `TimeEntry` gained two required fields: `pausedForResume: boolean` (true when an entry was closed by Pause, or by auto-pause when a different timer started, rather than by an explicit Stop — controls whether Resume is offered for it) and `continuesFromEntryId: string | null` (set only on an entry created by Resume, pointing at the task's own last paused entry — chains a "working session" across pause boundaries). Pause and Stop both finalize a running entry through the same path Stop already used (`minutesBetween`); they differ only in `pausedForResume`. `resumeTimer` never mutates the paused entry — it creates a **new** entry on that entry's own `taskId`, `continuesFromEntryId` pointing back at it, after auto-pausing (renamed from auto-stopping) whatever else the user has running. **Resumability is scoped per-task, not global**: a task's own latest entry having `pausedForResume === true` drives that task's own Resume button; a separate global "most-recently-paused-anywhere" query (`getPausedTimer`) only drives a soft cross-task hint elsewhere (My Day) and is explicitly non-authoritative. Pausing Task A, working Task B, then resuming Task A correctly **continues A's existing chain** — `continuesFromEntryId` only ever links two entries of the same task, so B's activity never enters A's chain. A new pure helper, `sumChainMinutes(entries, fromEntryId)` (`src/lib/data/time-entry-chain.ts`), walks the chain backward through an already-fetched entries array to total prior accumulated time; `useElapsedSeconds` gained a `baseSeconds` param so a resumed timer's live counter continues from that total instead of restarting at zero.

**2. Expected-time input bug, fixed.** Root-caused via direct reproduction: focusing an empty `type="number"` field flips its DOM `.value` to `"0"` before any keystroke, so typed digits get prepended-to rather than replacing (arrows worked fine — the browser parsed the underlying number correctly; only the on-screen string was wrong). Fixed in `expected-time-input.tsx` by switching to `type="text" inputMode="decimal"` with a regex-gated (`/^\d*\.?\d*$/`) `handleAmountChange` — the standard React pattern for free-typed numeric text, at the cost of the native spin buttons (an acceptable tradeoff; the unit `Select` already provides coarse adjustment).

**3. Estimate vs. actual, per task.** `computeWorkstreamBudget` (renamed module, see "Time vs. Budget" above) was already domain-generic, so `TaskTimeTracking` (task detail page) now feeds it the task's own `expectedMinutes` plus its already-computed actual/billable/non-billable minutes and renders the same `BudgetBar` used at the workstream/client level — no duplicated comparison logic. The client-level rollup (company detail page) needed no changes at all; it already summed workstream budgets, and this phase only added the task-level leaf.

**4. Team Time — new page.** `/dashboard/team-time`, a structural twin of Team Updates (Phase 3.24): a roster (from `assignableStaffFor`) + `DateStepper` drive a detail pane, via one new provider method, `listTimeEntriesForDate(viewer, date)`, that returns everyone visible for that day in a single call (filtered day-bucket + `canViewTimeForUser`). Unlike Team Updates' 1:1 person→entry mapping, entries are grouped per person since multiple time entries per person per day are normal. The roster row shows a running/logged/no-time status dot and that day's total (with a trailing "+" if still running); the detail pane shows a total/billable-split summary and each entry (task, Running…/Paused badge, billable badge, time range, notes). A soft "N logged across the team" stat chip appears only when there's something to show — deliberately not a "logged today" headcount, which would read as attendance-checking. **Visibility**: `canViewTeamTimePage(user) = isSupervisor(user) || isSuperadmin(user)` gates the page/sidebar item; `canViewTimeForUser(viewer, targetUserId, allUsers)` (own time always visible, someone else's only to whoever manages them — a structural twin of `canViewDailyUpdate`) gates each entry the provider method returns. Employees have no access — their own time lives on My Day and the task detail page only, same posture as Team Updates.

**5. My Day "Today" card — Pause/Resume wired in.** The existing total + billable/non-billable split already was the "presence/effort sense" the request asked for — no framing change, just new button states (Pause+Stop when running, Resume when paused, cross-task hints naming the other task by title). Extracted into a shared `components/my-day/today-time-card.tsx` (previously triplicated verbatim across the three role-specific My Day files) to avoid the new pause/resume/chain-walking logic drifting across three copies.

**Verified** via Playwright across all three roles, light and dark mode: pause freezes the elapsed display and offers Resume; resuming continues the counter from the paused total rather than restarting at zero; starting a second task's timer auto-pauses (not stops) the first, which correctly shows Resume when revisited, with no cross-task "running elsewhere" hint once a task's own state is "paused here"; a task's own budget bar renders the correct under/near/over status once an estimate is set; typing a fractional expected-time value character-by-character now displays exactly what was typed; Team Time's roster is correctly scoped per role (superadmin sees everyone, supervisor sees self + direct reports only, employees have no sidebar entry at all) and its detail pane's totals/entries match seed data exactly.

**Not built this phase** (explicitly out of scope per the request): geolocation/clock-in-out, idle detection/screenshots/activity monitoring, and any AI auto-generation of time entries or summaries.

## Time UI Clarity + Workstream/Activity Hierarchy (Phase 3.33)

Five independent fixes/additions, bundled since all five were requested together: a real input bug, a genuinely confusing time display, a workflow gap in task creation, a navigation bug masquerading as a data bug, and a data-model narrowing to match the confirmed **Client → Workstream (service) → Activity → Task** hierarchy.

**1. Expected-time input — typing plus steppers.** Free typing was already fixed in Phase 3.32 (`type="text" inputMode="decimal"`, verified still working here); this phase added back up/down stepper buttons (±1 in whatever unit is currently showing, never below 0) without reintroducing the native `type="number"` bug they were originally dropped to avoid. **Caught and fixed during verification**: the first pass absolutely-positioned the buttons on top of the input, which on this field's actual rendered width (~44px — the fixed-width unit `Select` was eating nearly all of a cramped 2-column grid cell) covered most of the input's own clickable area, silently blocking clicks into it. Fixed two ways: the stepper is now a proper adjacent sibling (a bordered column butted against the input's right edge, native-spinner-style) rather than an overlay, so it can never cover the input's own hit area regardless of width; and the "Expected time" field in both the task form and workstream form now spans the full row (`sm:col-span-2`) instead of being squeezed into half a 2-column grid, giving the compound value+steppers+unit control real room.

**2. Time section redesign — Estimated/Actual, one shared unit, two labeled bars.** The task detail page's time card read as "3.7h logged of 1 day expected — 46%" with two unlabeled stacked bars — confusing because (a) the two numbers independently best-fit to different units (`formatExpectedTime` picks the roundest unit *per value*, so an estimate entered as "1 day" and an actual of ~220 minutes rendered in different units instead of a shared one) and (b) neither bar said what it was. Fixed at the source, in the shared `TimeBudget`/`BudgetBar` (`time-budget.ts`/`ui/budget-bar.tsx`) rather than a task-only special case, since the identical bug existed at the workstream/client-rollup level too:
- `TimeBudget` gained minute-precision totals (`actualMinutes`/`billableMinutes`/`nonBillableMinutes`, alongside the existing Hours fields) so a display can pick one common unit for both sides of a comparison instead of each independently best-fitting.
- `BudgetBar`'s headline is now literally "Estimated: **{X}** · Actual: **{Y}** — {percent}%, {status}" — both `X`/`Y` computed via a new `formatMinutesAs(minutes, unit)` (`expected-time.ts`) in one shared unit, anchored to whatever unit the estimate itself was entered in (`bestUnitFor(expectedMinutes)`) — so "1 day" now consistently reads as "Actual: 0.44 days," never "3.7h," while the person's own estimate always displays exactly as they entered it.
- Both bars are now explicitly labeled with a small mono micro-header: **"Time budget"** above the estimate-vs-actual progress bar (green under / amber near / red over, unchanged thresholds), **"Billable split"** above the second bar — which also changed color from the primary accent to `bg-info` (blue) specifically so "billable vs. non-billable" reads as a visually distinct signal from the budget bar's own status color, not a second budget indicator.
- `BudgetBarCompact` (used wherever workstreams are listed) got the identical shared-unit fix.
- `TaskTimeTracking` (the task detail page's time card) was restructured into three explicitly labeled zones, each its own bordered `bg-muted/20` block (the same "secondary but visible" treatment the Attention Strip established in Phase 3.16): **Timer** (Start/Pause/Resume/Stop, live elapsed, cross-task hints, "Log time") — i.e. the actual mechanism for recording time; **Estimate vs. actual** (the relabeled `BudgetBar`, only rendered when the task has an estimate) — the comparison; **Time entries** (the existing per-entry log list) — the raw history. This is a pure display reorganization; no provider/permission logic changed, and it stays visually and structurally separate from the Details card's own checklist-completion bar, which was already a different `Card` before this phase.

**3. "+ New workstream" inline from task creation.** A supervisor/superadmin creating a task can now click a small "+ New workstream" link next to the Workstream field (visible once a specific client is picked — a workstream needs a company to belong to, same constraint the standalone form already had) to open the *exact same* `WorkstreamFormDialog` nested inside the task panel — the identical "nested dialog inside a dialog" shape Phase 2.7's Reuse-from-Past picker already established inside this same form. `WorkstreamFormDialog` gained an optional `onCreated(workstream)` callback (fired only on create, never edit) so the task form can select the brand-new workstream directly from the callback's own return value — no need to wait for a refetch to find it in a list.

Effort/estimate/due-date belong on the Tasks living inside it.

No checklist field ever existed on Workstream (verified directly), and `startDate`/`endDate` were already optional — the task-like feel was pure wording, fixed by relabeling rather than a data change: "End date" → **"Renewal date"** (form and detail page), and `WorkstreamOverviewCard`'s literal `Due {date}` badge → **"Renews {date}"**. The field is still named `endDate` internally (touches fewer files, no behavior change) — a future rename is cheap to do later if wanted.

**Time vs. Budget roll-up now sourced from tasks, not a workstream field** — see the updated "Time vs. Budget" section above for the mechanics (`workstreamHours()` sums the workstream's own tasks' `expectedMinutes`). Two call sites that used to push a workstream-level estimate stopped: Apply Template (still shows the same sum as an informational preview figure, just doesn't write it anywhere) and Generate Next Occurrence (dropped its "carried over" line entirely — each cloned task already carries its own estimate forward, so the new workstream's roll-up is correct automatically).

## Task Assignment & Status Notifications (Phase 3.35)

Closes a gap found during the Phase 3 Supervisor/Employee RBAC ground-truth audit: `task-assigned` and `task-status-changed` had been declared `NotificationType` values (with UI labels already wired in Settings → Notifications) since early on, but nothing anywhere ever created one — an assignee was never actually told they'd been assigned, and nobody was told when a task's status changed. Both are now wired, reusing the exact same local-helper-plus-`db.notifications.push` pattern `notifyOfSelfAddedTask`/`notifyOfHandoff` already established — no new provider methods, no schema change, no change to the RBAC rules the audit had just verified (`resolveAssigneeIds`, `canProgressTask`, etc. are all untouched).

**Task assigned**: fires only for genuinely *new* assignees — on create, every initial assignee except the actor themselves (so a self-added task, whose sole assignee is always its own creator, correctly produces zero `task-assigned` notifications, only the pre-existing `self-added-task` one); on edit, only the assignees added since the previous save (diffed against the task's assignee set as it stood immediately before the edit), never the ones who were already on it. An edit that doesn't touch assignees at all produces zero new assignment notifications. The actor is never notified about assigning themselves.

**Task status changed**: deliberately restrained, not an audit log — recipients are the task's other current assignees (never the actor about their own change) plus, only when the actor is an employee, that employee's own supervisor (`viewer.supervisorId`), so the one person responsible for their work hears about it. Superadmins are never added automatically. Fires from all three places a status can actually change — `updateTaskStatus`, a full `updateTask` edit that happens to change status, and the checklist-driven auto-Done/auto-revert transition (Phase 3.31) — each gated on "did the status actually change," so a save that leaves status untouched never fires one. The checklist path has exactly one call site inside `toggleChecklistItem`, itself gated behind the same "did this toggle actually flip the task's status" check the auto-Done logic already used, so a single checklist toggle can never double-fire.

**Verified** via Playwright across Supervisor/Employee, with real seed accounts (Priya Nair supervises Alicia Chen and Sam Torres): Priya assigning a task to Alicia notified only Alicia, not herself, not Sam, not an unrelated supervisor; adding Sam as a second assignee afterward notified only Sam, leaving Alicia's existing notification untouched (no duplicate); a no-op edit (touching only the description) produced zero new notifications for either; Alicia changing the task's own status herself correctly notified both Priya (her supervisor) and Sam (the other assignee), never Alicia herself; ticking the last checklist item on a seeded task auto-completed it and produced exactly one status-change notification for the assignee, and unticking one afterward correctly reverted the status and produced exactly one more (distinct) notification — no duplicates in either direction. Every notification's link lands the recipient on a task they already have access to, since `notificationHref` routes any non-report type through `/dashboard/tasks/{relatedTaskId}` unchanged.

**Notification access fix**: manual testing surfaced a real gap — `canAccessTask` requires an employee to both be a direct assignee *and* have the task's company in their own `assignedCompanyIds` (unchanged, intentional RBAC), but the two notify functions above fired off assignment alone, with no check that the recipient could actually pass that second gate. An employee assigned to a task outside their own `assignedCompanyIds` got a notification whose link then dead-ended on an access-denied page. Fixed with a `notifiableRecipients()` filter (`canAccessTask`-checked) applied to both `notifyOfAssignment` and `notifyOfStatusChange` before a notification is ever created — who's conceptually supposed to be notified is unchanged, this just guarantees the link always resolves. No change to `canAccessTask`, `notificationHref`, or My Day. Re-verified manually afterward: the Employee task-assigned notification opens the referenced task directly, and the status-change/checklist-auto-Done/revert notifications above still work with no duplicates.

**Also re-confirmed manually this round**: the Phase 3.33 inline "+ New workstream" flow from Task creation (Supervisor) — client selected, new workstream created under it without leaving the task form, auto-selected back on the task, and the Activity picker correctly re-scoped to the new workstream's service line (e.g. a Payroll workstream showing only Payroll activities). No code changes were needed here; this was investigated as a suspected regression and confirmed working as designed.

**Not built this phase**: no change to who can be assigned or how (`resolveAssigneeIds`, `assignableStaffFor` untouched); no notification on task creation/edit fields other than assignment and status (title, due date, etc. stay silent, matching how this was scoped as closing exactly two dead notification types, not a general activity feed).
